🏙️ CITY GUIDES

Where to actually go first

CT
City Tech Team
Six cities · Updated November 2026

There is no shortage of "top 10 things to do in Shanghai" articles. These are not those. Each city guide here answers the questions that actually stop foreign visitors: which payment method the city runs on, how its metro charges you, where your passport becomes a problem, and what two or three days realistically covers. Pick the city, get the practical layer, then go enjoy it.

🛂 Before you pick a city
Two visa routes changed recently and both are generous: 240-hour (10-day) transit visa-free now covers 57 countries through 65 ports, and unilateral 30-day visa-free covers 45 countries until 31 December 2026. Transit visa-free requires an onward ticket to a third country — check your own case before booking. See arrival and immigration.
BeijingImperial capital
🏯

The big-ticket history, and the most bureaucratic city in China. Everything works — you just have to book it in advance.

  • Forbidden City sells out on a passport-only booking system
  • Great Wall: three sections, very different realities
  • Runs on Alipay; metro takes QR directly
Read the Beijing guide ›
ShanghaiEasiest first stop
🌆

The softest landing in mainland China. Every chain accepts foreign cards, English is survivable, nothing is shut on a Monday.

  • Metro English signage on every line
  • Maglev from Pudong airport in 8 minutes
  • Best city for a jet-lagged first 48 hours
Read the Shanghai guide ›
Xi'anTerracotta Army
🗿

One world-class site, one intact city wall, and the best street food in the country. Two days, done properly.

  • Terracotta Army needs advance passport booking
  • Muslim Quarter is a night-time city
  • The wall is 14km — rent a bike
Read the Xi'an guide ›
ChongqingVertical city
🌉

A city with no flat ground, where a monorail goes through a building and "ground floor" is a meaningless term. Bring good shoes.

  • Metro exits can be 10 floors off street level
  • Hongyadong is a 11-storey building, not a street
  • Hotpot is the reason you came
Read the Chongqing guide ›
ChengduPandas & teahouses
🐼

The one Chinese city where the point is to slow down. Pandas at dawn, teahouses by noon, hotpot after dark.

  • Panda base: arrive at opening, not at 10am
  • Teahouse culture you can actually join
  • Gateway to Sichuan's mountains
Read the Chengdu guide ›
GuangzhouCantonese south
🥟

Dim sum at 7am, a subtropical riverfront, and the fastest route to Hong Kong. The most underrated city on this list.

  • Morning tea is a ritual, not a meal
  • High-speed rail to Hong Kong in under an hour
  • Cantonese spoken first, Mandarin second
Read the Guangzhou guide ›
📝 The practical layer applies everywhere
Whichever city you pick, three guides do most of the work before you land: setting up Alipay, getting data on your phone, and the pre-trip checklist. Do those first and the city guides become optional reading rather than essential survival.
