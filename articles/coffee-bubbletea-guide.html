How to Order Coffee & Bubble Tea in China: Starbucks, Luckin, HeyTea & More (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

China drinks more coffee than you think — Luckin alone serves 5+ million cups a day — and bubble tea (boba / nǎichá) is genuinely a national obsession, consumed the way Italians drink espresso. On any given block in Shanghai or Chengdu you'll pass three bubble tea shops and one coffee chain, and all of them are packed.

For a foreigner, ordering can be confusing: the menu is in Chinese, you order by scanning a QR code, and the customization options (sugar level? ice level? cheese foam?) don't translate cleanly. This guide covers how to order at the four major chains without stress.

Coffee chains

☕ Starbucks China (星巴克)

Easiest for foreigners. Nearly identical to Starbucks anywhere — same menu structure, same sizes (Tall/Grande/Venti), most staff speak basic English in tier-1 cities. The key differences:

  • The app has English. Download "Starbucks China" (星巴克中国) → register with your phone → switch to English in Settings → order ahead. Your foreign card works through Alipay.
  • The menu has more tea and food options than Western Starbucks. The "Teavana" section is extensive.
  • Same prices as your home country (¥30-40 for a latte — about $4-5.50). Not cheap by Chinese standards, but exactly what you expect.
  • If all else fails, say "latte" and show one finger. It works.

☕ Luckin Coffee (瑞幸) — the cheap, everywhere option

Luckin is China's homegrown Starbucks-killer. There are 20,000+ locations (that's about 5x Starbucks China), most are tiny pickup counters, and a latte costs ¥9.9 ($1.40) — one-third of Starbucks. The entire ordering system is app-based. No counter ordering. Here's how:

1
Download the Luckin app or use the WeChat mini-program

Search "Luckin" in WeChat's mini-program search. The app is faster.

2
The homepage shows a grid of drinks with photos and English names mixed in

Luckin menus are partially bilingual — core items have English subtitles (e.g., "生椰拿铁 / Coconut Latte," "美式 / Americano," "拿铁 / Latte"). The seasonal specials are usually Chinese-only, but the photos tell you what's in them.

3
Tap a drink → customize

Select your size (大杯 = large, 小杯 = small). Select sugar: 全糖 (full), 半糖 (half), 少糖 (less), 无糖 (none). Select temperature: 冰 (iced), 热 (hot), 少冰 (less ice), 去冰 (no ice). Skip anything you're unsure about — defaults are usually good.

4
Pay through Alipay or WeChat

¥9.9-18 ($1.40-$2.50) for most drinks. Coupons auto-load — Luckin's pricing model is built around discount coupons that appear in the app. You almost never pay the "sticker" price.

5
Wait 3-5 minutes, grab your drink from the counter

Your order number appears on a screen above the counter. No interaction needed — just walk up, check the number on the cup, and take it.

💡 Luckin's secret weapon
Luckin's "Coconut Latte" (生椰拿铁) is an accidental global hit — espresso + coconut milk. It's ¥9.9 and tastes better than a ¥35 Starbucks latte. Order one on your first day.

Bubble tea (boba / nǎichá) chains

China has dozens of bubble tea chains, but three dominate the tourist experience. All order via QR code at the counter or on your phone. None have real English menus, but they're visual enough that you can point your way through.

🟠

HeyTea (喜茶)

Price: ¥18-30 ($2.50-4). Known for: Cheese foam tea (芝士茶), fruit teas with real fruit chunks. Premium brand — long queues, Instagram-famous. Menu has photos. Order at counter or via QR scan on table. Ask for "zhīshì méiguī wūlóng" (cheese rose oolong) — the staff know it by this name.
🔵

Chagee (霸王茶姬)

Price: ¥15-25 ($2-3.50). Known for: Chinese tea-based milk teas with floral notes — jasmine, osmanthus, peach. Minimalist packaging (blue-and-white porcelain design). Best pure-tea flavor among the chains. "Bójué" (bergamot) and "Huātián" (flower field) are their best-known drinks.
🟢

Mixue (蜜雪冰城)

Price: ¥3-8 ($0.40-$1.10). Known for: Being absurdly cheap. 36,000+ locations — you'll see the snowman mascot on every block in small cities. Basic drinks: lemonade, simple milk tea, ice cream cones. Not premium quality, but ¥3 for an ice-cold lemon tea in summer is unbeatable value.

How to order bubble tea (the universal method)

1
Walk up to the counter or scan the QR code on the table

At HeyTea and Chagee, scan the table QR with Alipay/WeChat → the menu opens. At Mixue, order at the counter (they have picture menus).

2
Point at a picture on the menu

The top section is usually "招牌" (signature / recommended). These are the drinks the chain is famous for. Pointing at a photo of a drink you want works everywhere — staff are completely used to this.

3
Customize: sugar and ice level

This is where you need a few characters. Sugar: 全糖 (100% — very sweet), 半糖 (50%), 少糖 (30%), 微糖 (10%), 无糖 (0%). If you don't customize, the default is 100% — which is noticeably sweeter than Western palates expect. Recommend: choose 半糖 (50%) or 少糖 (30%). Ice: 正常冰 (normal), 少冰 (less), 去冰 (no ice).

4
Pay → wait for your number on the screen → grab it

HeyTea and Chagee have order-number screens. Mixue calls your number in Chinese. Look at your receipt for the 3-4 digit number and watch for it on the screen.

⚠️ The sweetness warning
Chinese bubble tea at 100% sugar is significantly sweeter than Western equivalents. A "full-sugar" HeyTea drink tastes like liquid candy. If you normally take 1-2 sugars in coffee, order 少糖 (30%) or 微糖 (10%). You can't change it after it's made.

Quick recommendations for first-timers

  • At Luckin: Coconut Latte (生椰拿铁) — ¥9.9. Creamy, slightly sweet, available hot or iced.
  • At HeyTea: Very Grape Cheese (多肉葡萄) — ¥29. Real grape chunks + cheese foam + jasmine tea base. Their signature drink.
  • At Chagee: White Peach Oolong Latte (白桃乌龙拿铁) — ¥18. Floral, delicate, not too sweet.
  • At Mixue: Lemon Water (柠檬水) — ¥4. It's exactly what it sounds like and it's ¥4.
  • At Starbucks: If you want something local, try the Dragon Dumpling (龙粽) seasonal or Mandarin Mascarpone cake from the pastry case.

Coffee and bubble tea in China aren't just drinks — they're the default social activity. Friends don't meet for a beer; they meet for nǎichá. At any hour of the day, any city, there's a bubble tea shop around the corner serving a ¥15 drink that's better than anything you've had at home. Learn the four characters for sugar level, scan a QR code, and join them.
