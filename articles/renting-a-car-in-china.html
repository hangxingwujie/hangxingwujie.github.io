🚗 DRIVING

Renting a Car in China: What Foreigners Actually Need (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated November 10, 2026 · 10 min read

Self-driving in China is legal, genuinely useful outside the big cities, and completely closed to you until you fix one problem: the driving licence you brought with you does not count here. Not your home licence on its own, and — the part that catches almost everyone — not your International Driving Permit.

This guide covers the permit that replaces it, how to get one in under an hour, what renting actually costs, which companies will rent to a foreigner, and an honest answer to whether a car is even the right call for your trip.

First: your IDP is legally worthless here

This is the single most important fact in this article. China is not a party to the 1949 Geneva Convention or the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic — the two treaties that give an International Driving Permit its legal force. Because China never signed either, an IDP issued anywhere in the world has no standing at a rental counter, a police checkpoint, or a Vehicle Management Office.

⚠️ Be sceptical of anyone selling you one
If a website or agency offers an "International Driving Permit valid for China," it is selling you a document that will not work. There is no legitimate IDP that overrides this. Do not budget for one, and do not bring one expecting it to unlock a rental car.

A related confusion worth clearing up: Hong Kong and Macao run separate systems that do accept IDPs. That recognition does not travel with you across the land border into the mainland.

The permit you actually need

Foreign visitors obtain a Chinese Temporary Driving Permit (临时机动车驾驶许可) in person at a local Vehicle Management Office (车管所) after arriving. It is issued on the spot, requires no written exam and no road test, and is the document that makes a rental legal.

Documents to bring

  • Passport with a valid visa or entry stamp. Your entry document is the anchor for everything else.
  • Your original home driving licence. Original, not a copy. It must be currently valid — an expired licence will stop the process dead.
  • A Chinese translation of that licence. If yours is not in Chinese, a translation is mandatory. Translation agencies handle this by email, or staff at the Vehicle Management Office counter can help you submit one.
  • A white-background passport-style photo. You can have one taken on site.
  • A Chinese address and phone number. Your hotel address and a contact number are normally enough. This trips up visitors who have not yet checked in, so sort your accommodation first.
1
Get your licence translated

Arrange the Chinese translation before you go, or ask the counter to help. This is the most common source of a second trip.

2
Go to a Vehicle Management Office

Any major city has one. Larger hubs including Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu and Chongqing have airport service counters, which is the most efficient option if you land and drive the same day.

3
Photo, forms, fee

They take your photo, you fill in the application, and you pay about ¥10.

4
Watch the safety video

Roughly 30 minutes on Chinese traffic law and road behaviour. Attendance is the requirement; there is no test afterwards.

5
Permit issued

Printed and handed over the same visit. Now you can legally rent and drive.

💡 Hainan is running a simplified pilot
On Hainan, authorities have simplified the process for short-stay visitors: no medical check required, and for stays beyond three months the permit can be valid up to one year (within your visa). Airport and port service counters have been added. If your trip is island-based, Hainan is currently the easiest place in the country to do this.

Which companies will actually rent to you

Plenty of small local rental firms will simply decline a foreign passport — not out of hostility, but because their counter staff cannot process the paperwork. Stick to the large chains.

CompanyForeigner-friendlyNotes
eHi (一嗨租车)★★★★★The most foreigner-friendly of the domestic chains. App or WeChat/Alipay mini-program. Registration typically wants a mainland phone number, so the mini-program route is easier. Verification usually clears within one working day.
CAR Inc. (神州租车)★★★★☆Largest domestic fleet, airport counters in most cities. Comparable experience to eHi.
Hertz / Avis China★★★★☆International branding and more English, but fewer locations, higher rates, and often operated by a local partner anyway.
Small local firms★☆☆☆☆Frequently refuse foreign licences outright. Not worth the risk on a short trip.

You will need to upload your passport, visa and Temporary Driving Permit for identity verification before the booking is confirmed. Doing this the night before, rather than at the counter, is the difference between a ten-minute pickup and an hour of standing around.

What it really costs

Daily rates look reasonable. The deposit and the extras are where budgets go wrong.

  • Rental rate: roughly ¥230–440 per day ($32–62) for an economy or mid-size car with basic insurance included.
  • Insurance upgrades: zero-deductible cover adds about ¥60–100/day; personal accident cover about ¥20–40/day. On Chinese roads, the zero-deductible cover is worth taking.
  • Deposit: ¥3,000–10,000 ($420–1,400) pre-authorised on a credit card, and it can stay held for up to 30 days after you return the car. This is the number that surprises people — it is a genuine hit to your available credit, not a small hold.
  • Tolls: ¥0.4–0.6 per kilometre on expressways. A long road trip can add 30–50% on top of rental and fuel. Budget for it explicitly.
  • Fuel: paid by you, and the car should go back at the level it left with.
  • Parking: the quiet budget killer in city centres, where hourly rates are steep and spaces are scarce.
⚠️ The deposit is a real constraint
Foreign renters generally must pre-authorise the deposit on a Visa or Mastercard credit card in their own name. Some operators accept a deposit-free rental using a WeChat Pay or Alipay credit-score authorisation instead, and a domestic friend can pay a deposit on a UnionPay card at the counter. A foreign debit card will usually not work. Confirm the deposit method with the specific branch before you rely on it.

Pickup and return without drama

  • Photograph everything before you drive off. Walk the car and film a slow lap: every panel, all four wheels, the windscreen, the fuel gauge, the odometer. Chinese rental disputes over pre-existing damage are the most common complaint, and your video is the only thing that settles it.
  • Check the fuel policy. Most contracts are same-to-same. Returning with less fuel incurs a refuelling charge at a marked-up rate.
  • Ask where the toll transponder is. Most cars carry an ETC unit for automatic expressway payment; the charge is settled at return. If the car has none, you will queue at manual toll booths with cash.
  • Language: airport counters at eHi and CAR Inc. usually manage basic English, but city branches often do not. Have your booking number, passport and permit ready, and use a translation app for anything complicated.
  • Return on time. Late fees are charged per hour and escalate quickly.

On the road

  • Navigation: use Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps. Google Maps is unreliable in China — the data is stale and routing is poor. Amap has an English interface and is the better pick for drivers.
  • Drive on the right. Same side as the US and Europe, opposite to the UK, Australia, Japan and India.
  • Blood alcohol limit is 0.02% — effectively zero tolerance, among the strictest anywhere. One drink is not worth the risk; penalties are severe and include detention.
  • Speed cameras are everywhere and enforcement is automated. Urban limits are commonly 40–60 km/h, expressways 100–120 km/h. Fines are typically collected at return or charged to the card on file.
  • Expect checkpoints on expressways and near provincial borders. Keep your passport, permit and rental contract in the car — not in your luggage in the boot.
  • Parking: mall and hotel car parks are the reliable option in cities. On-street parking is scarce and often restricted by time.

Where you cannot self-drive

⚠️ Two regions have separate rules
Tibet requires an organised tour with a licensed guide and pre-arranged permits. Independent foreign self-driving is not possible there, whatever permit you hold elsewhere in China.
Xinjiang is driveable but heavily checkpounted; expect frequent stops and allow substantially more time than the distance suggests.

Honest verdict: is a car the right choice?

Not always, and most guides oversell it. The trade-off is genuinely three-way.

OptionBest forWhy not always
Rental carRural routes, national parks, small towns, familiesPermit admin, large deposit, city parking, language barrier at the counter
High-speed railCity-to-city movementGets you between cities brilliantly; leaves you stranded once you leave the station
Private driverDay trips, groups, zero-admin travelCosts more per day than a rental, but no permit, no deposit, no navigation

The pattern that works: use rail to move between cities, and rent a car (or hire a driver) only for the segment where you actually need wheels — a two-day loop through rice terraces, a national park, a coastal road. Renting a car for a Beijing–Shanghai–Xi'an itinerary is paying a premium to sit in traffic and hunt for parking.

And if you are only in the country for a week, inside major cities, the honest answer is that you do not need a car at all. Metro plus DiDi covers it for less money and far less friction.

Before you book

  1. Confirm your home licence is valid and not expiring during the trip.
  2. Do not buy an IDP for China. It will not be accepted.
  3. Arrange a Chinese translation of your licence before you fly.
  4. Book accommodation first — you need a Chinese address and phone for the permit.
  5. Plan to get the Temporary Driving Permit on arrival; allow an hour.
  6. Check which Vehicle Management Office or airport counter is nearest your arrival point.
  7. Confirm the deposit method with the specific rental branch, and make sure your Visa/Mastercard has the headroom.
  8. Complete the rental company's identity verification online the night before pickup.
  9. Install Amap before you leave the airport Wi-Fi.
  10. Budget expressway tolls at ¥0.4–0.6/km, not just the daily rate.

Do the permit, do the translation, do the deposit check — and China by car is one of the most rewarding ways to see the country, mostly because almost no other foreign visitor is doing it.
