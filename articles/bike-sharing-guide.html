China Bike-Sharing Guide: Meituan vs Hellobike vs DiDi Qingju (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Every sidewalk in every Chinese city is lined with brightly colored bicycles — yellow, blue, green. They're dockless bike-shares, and they cost about ¥1-3 ($0.15-0.40) per ride. You unlock one with your phone, ride to wherever you're going, park it neatly on the sidewalk, and walk away. No docking station, no key, no deposit (for most), no Chinese skills required.

Bike-sharing is the single cheapest way to cover the 1-3 km distance that's too far to walk and too short for a DiDi. Between a metro station and a restaurant. Along a riverside path. Through a hutong neighborhood. This guide covers the three major platforms and how to ride without a single Chinese word.

ℹ️ No app download required (mostly)
You can unlock all three major bike-share brands directly through Alipay or WeChat — scan the QR code on the bike with the app's built-in scanner, and it opens a mini-program that handles unlocking and payment. No separate app to install. But the standalone apps have better maps and features if you ride a lot.

The three major platforms

🟡

Meituan Bike (美团单车)

Yellow bikes. The most common in most cities. Scan with WeChat or Meituan app. ¥1.5 per 30 minutes. Monthly pass available (~¥15-25/month unlimited).
🔵

Hellobike (哈啰单车)

Blue bikes with white accents. Strong in tier-2/3 cities. Scan with Alipay or Hellobike app. ¥1.5 per 30 min. Often has new-user free ride promos — check the mini-program homepage.
🟢

DiDi Qingju (滴滴青桔)

Green bikes. DiDi's bike brand. Scan with DiDi app → switch to "Bike" tab. ¥1.5 per 30 min. Convenient if you already use DiDi for taxis — one less mini-program to navigate.

How to unlock a bike

1
Find a bike

They're everywhere on sidewalks and near metro exits. Look for yellow (Meituan), blue (Hellobike), or green (DiDi Qingju). The bike's color is your platform clue.

2
Open Alipay or WeChat, tap Scan

Each bike has a QR code on the handlebar or behind the seat. Point your Alipay/WeChat scanner at it. A mini-program opens.

3
Grant permissions (first time only)

The mini-program asks for Bluetooth and location access. Allow both — Bluetooth is how the bike lock communicates.

4
Tap "Unlock" (开锁)

The lock clicks open. The bike is yours. A timer starts tracking your ride for billing.

5
At your destination: park properly, then manually close the lock

Push the lock mechanism closed by hand. You'll hear a beep. The app automatically ends the ride and charges your Alipay/WeChat.

⚠️ Pay attention to parking zones
In city centers, bikes must be parked inside marked zones (painted rectangles on the sidewalk, or indicated in the app map). If you park outside a zone, the app may charge a penalty (¥5-20) or refuse to end the ride. The mini-program map shows parking areas — look for blue-shaded regions on the map after you scan. If no restrictions show, park neatly against the curb and don't block pedestrian flow.

Payment and pricing

Standard rate: ¥1.5 per 30 minutes on all three platforms. If you ride for 25 minutes: ¥1.5. For 40 minutes: ¥3. For an hour: ¥3. The billing is precise — the app knows exactly when you unlocked and relocked.

  • Payment: Charges to your Alipay or WeChat balance (which pulls from your linked foreign card). Automatic — no checkout step.
  • Monthly/weekly pass: If you're riding every day, buy a pass: ~¥15-25/month for unlimited 30-min rides (overtime still charges). Available in the mini-program under "我的卡" or "VIP." These are the best value if you ride 10+ times.
  • Deposits: Historically, bike-shares in China required deposits. As of 2024-2026, none of the three major platforms require a deposit — they bill per ride through your Alipay/WeChat wallet. No deposit, no pre-load.

Which one should you use?

The honest answer: whichever color bike is physically closest to you. The three platforms are nearly identical in price, reliability, and user experience. The bikes are all basic single-speed city bikes with a basket, a bell, and built-in wheel lock. There's no meaningful quality difference.

That said:

  • In big cities (Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen): Meituan yellow bikes dominate — you'll see 5 yellows for every 1 blue or green.
  • In smaller cities: Hellobike blue bikes are more common.
  • If you already use DiDi for taxis: Qingju green is convenient — one fewer mini-program.
  • If you have the Meituan food-delivery app: Meituan Bike is already installed.
💡 Scan with Alipay — it works for all three
Alipay's built-in scanner recognizes all three brands. You don't need to identify the brand before scanning — just point at any bike QR code and Alipay figures it out.

Riding etiquette and safety

  • Bike lanes exist everywhere. Most major roads in Chinese cities have separated bike lanes between the sidewalk and the car lane. Use them — you're much safer and cars can't enter them.
  • Helmets: Nobody wears one. There's no helmet law for shared bikes. Use your best judgment.
  • Traffic patterns: Scooters and e-bikes share the bike lane and they're silent. They appear behind you with no warning. Ride predictably, don't weave, and check before changing direction. An electric scooter doing 40 km/h from behind is the #1 urban cycling hazard.
  • One-way streets: Apply to bikes too. The police don't usually enforce this on shared bikes, but cars turning at intersections may not expect a cyclist coming from the wrong direction.
  • Rain: Bikes have no fenders. Your back will get wet. Plastic ponchos are sold at convenience stores (¥10) — buy one if it's drizzling.

Parking rules (don't get fined)

  • Do: Park in marked bike zones, against the curb, without blocking pedestrian flow. Rows of other parked bikes are always a safe spot.
  • Don't: Park in the middle of a sidewalk, blocking a store entrance, on a road, inside a gated community, or any obviously obstructed spot. The app knows your GPS location and can flag illegal parking.
  • Fines: First offense is usually a warning. Repeated bad parking can trigger a ¥5-20 fee or even a temporary ban from the platform.

Quick checklist

  1. Set up Alipay before your trip — it's your bike key
  2. Find a yellow/blue/green bike on any sidewalk
  3. Scan the QR code with Alipay's scanner (works for all brands)
  4. Allow Bluetooth + location on first scan
  5. Park against the curb, in a marked zone, manually close the lock
  6. For daily use, buy a monthly pass (~¥15-25, pays off after 10 rides)

Bike-sharing is one of the great urban inventions of the last decade, and nowhere does it better than China. You land, you set up Alipay, you scan a yellow bike outside your hotel, and you're exploring the city at bicycle speed for less than the cost of a bottle of water.
