Can I Use My Phone in China? Roaming vs eSIM vs Local SIM (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated August 5, 2026 · 9 min read

Yes — your phone works in China. The real question is how you want to get data: international roaming on your home plan, an eSIM you install before you fly, or a local SIM from an airport counter. All three connect you to the same Chinese mobile networks (China Mobile, Unicom, and Telecom). They differ in price, setup time, whether you keep your phone number, and what you can actually open once you land.

This guide compares all three head-to-head — including the traps that catch first-timers, like US eSIM-only iPhones and roaming day passes that quietly cost $12 a day.

Quick answer

🔍 30-second version
Roaming = most expensive, zero setup, your usual apps usually work, you keep your number.
eSIM = best for most travelers: install it at home on Wi-Fi, costs $12–25 for a week of data, data-only.
Local SIM = cheapest for long stays, but you wait in a passport line at the airport and the domestic network doesn't give you your usual apps by default.

Our pick for most trips: an eSIM, installed before you fly.

Option 1: International roaming (your home carrier)

Roaming means your existing carrier gives you data through a partner network in China. You do nothing before you fly — the moment you land, your phone connects and your home plan bills you.

  • Cost: US carriers typically charge $10–15 per day (AT&T International Day Pass and Verizon TravelPass are around $12/day; T-Mobile includes 5GB of high-speed data in China on most plans, then throttles). European carriers often include China in regional roaming bundles or charge roughly £6–10/day. Check your plan before you assume.
  • Setup: none. It just works.
  • Your number: yes — inbound texts and calls arrive on your normal number, which is exactly what you want for banking 2FA codes.
  • Your usual apps: roaming data generally routes back through your home network, so services you use at home — Google Maps, WhatsApp, Instagram — tend to work normally while roaming in China.
⚠️ The day-pass trap
A 10-day trip at $12/day is $120 — more than double an eSIM. And if you don't switch on a day pass, you can get billed per megabyte. If you choose roaming, set up the day pass in your carrier app before departure and set a data-usage alert.

Option 2: eSIM (install before you fly) — our pick

An eSIM is a digital SIM you buy from an international provider (we've tested Airalo, Nomad, Saily, and Holafly in our China eSIM comparison) and install on your phone while you still have Wi-Fi at home. On landing, you switch your data line to the eSIM and you're online.

  • Cost: China data plans run roughly $3–7 per GB, or $5–10/day for unlimited plans. A typical 5–10GB plan for a one-to-two-week trip costs $12–25.
  • Setup: 5–10 minutes, done before you fly.
  • Your number: no — eSIM plans are data-only. Keep your home SIM active for SMS (disable its data roaming so it doesn't bill you).
  • Your usual apps: this varies by provider and plan — some China plans include international routing so your usual apps work, others don't. We cover exactly which ones in the eSIM comparison.
1
Check eSIM support

On iPhone: Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM. On Android: Settings → Connections → SIM manager (most flagships from 2020 onward support eSIM).

2
Buy a China plan

Pick a provider and data package in our comparison. 5GB is comfortable for a week of maps, messaging, and QR payments.

3
Install while on Wi-Fi

Scan the QR code or tap install in the provider's app. The eSIM activates instantly; you don't need to wait for landing.

4
Switch data lines at the airport

Turn off data roaming on your home SIM and set the eSIM as your cellular data line. Calls and SMS still come through your home number.

5
Top up in the app

Run low? Most providers let you add data in-app in seconds — no airport counters involved.

💡 Why eSIM wins for most travelers
It's the only option that's cheap, zero-friction (no passport queue), keeps your home number for 2FA, and works in US eSIM-only iPhones. The only requirement is 5 minutes on Wi-Fi before you fly.

Option 3: Local Chinese SIM (airport counter)

China Mobile, Unicom, and Telecom all run kiosks at major international airports (Pudong, Capital, Daxing, and most regional hubs). You hand over your passport, pick a prepaid data package, and walk out with a working SIM.

  • Cost: tourist data packages run roughly ¥100–300 (about $14–42) for 7–30 days with 10–30GB — the cheapest per GB of the three options.
  • Setup: 20–30 minutes at the counter, passport required.
  • Your number: yes — you get a Chinese number, useful for restaurant waitlists and as a backup for ride-hailing apps.
  • Your usual apps: a local SIM connects to the domestic network, so international services generally don't work without additional setup. If you go this route, read our internet access guide before you land.
⚠️ Won't fit in a US eSIM-only iPhone
US iPhone 14 and newer models have no physical SIM slot. If that's your phone, the local SIM option is off the table — use roaming or an eSIM instead.

Will my phone even work in China?

Three compatibility questions worth checking before you fly:

  • Is it unlocked? Unlocked phones work with roaming, eSIM, and local SIMs. Carrier-locked phones (common in the US) can roam on your home plan, but can't take a local SIM or a third-party eSIM. If you're not sure, check with your carrier — most will unlock after the contract term.
  • eSIM-only phone? US iPhone 14 and newer have no physical SIM slot. That means: roaming and eSIM work, local SIMs don't. If you bought your phone in the US in 2022 or later, this is you.
  • Bands and dual SIM: Recent iPhones and Androids cover the LTE/5G bands used in China (LTE 1/3/5/8/38/39/40/41; 5G n1/n28/n41/n78/n79) and support dual SIM. The ideal setup: eSIM or local SIM for data + your home SIM for SMS — turn off data roaming on the home line so you only pay for the 2FA texts you receive.

Which one should you pick?

1
1–4 day business trip

Use roaming if your plan already includes China (T-Mobile, many EU plans) — zero setup beats saving $40. Otherwise grab an eSIM.

2
First trip, 1–2 weeks

eSIM, installed at home before departure. Pick a plan with international routing from our comparison so your usual apps work.

3
2+ weeks or heavy data

Local SIM from the airport (cheapest per GB), or an eSIM with a large data package if you're on an eSIM-only phone. Keep your home SIM active for 2FA either way.

4
Multi-country Asia trip

Pick a regional eSIM covering China, Japan, South Korea, and Southeast Asia in one plan — cheaper than separate SIMs per country.

Quick FAQs

  • Will Google Maps and WhatsApp work? With roaming, usually yes. With an eSIM, it depends on the plan (some route internationally, some don't — see the eSIM guide). With a local SIM, no by default.
  • Do I need a VPN with a local SIM? If you want to use the same services you do at home, you may need a connectivity service on top. Everything you need to know is in our staying-connected guide.
  • Will I still receive bank SMS codes? Yes — as long as your home SIM is active, SMS arrives even while roaming (receiving texts is usually free or cheap). This is why we recommend keeping it on with data roaming off.
  • Is airport Wi-Fi enough? It's fine for messaging and maps in the terminal, but QR-code payments, ride-hailing, and restaurant ordering all need cellular data once you're outside.

Before you fly — checklist

  1. Confirm your phone is unlocked and (if eSIM) supports eSIM.
  2. Decide roaming vs eSIM before departure — don't decide at the airport gate.
  3. If eSIM: buy and install your plan while on Wi-Fi at home.
  4. Keep your home SIM active for 2FA; turn off its data roaming.
  5. Download offline maps and a Chinese language pack for your translate app.
  6. Save your hotel's address in Chinese characters for taxi drivers.
