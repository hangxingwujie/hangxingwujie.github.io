🏨 HOTELS

China Hotel Check-In Guide for Foreigners (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

You land, you book a hotel online, you show up at the front desk, you show your passport, you get your key. It should be the same process as anywhere else. But in China, there are a few specific rules and surprises that catch foreign guests off — from the "foreign guest eligible" licensing requirement to deposits paid by mobile QR, from police registration to why the receptionist is photographing your passport.

This guide covers what to know before booking, at check-in, during your stay, and at check-out.

Before you book: the most important rule

Not every hotel in China is licensed to accept foreign guests. This is the single biggest trap for first-time visitors. A hotel can refuse to check you in — even with a confirmed, paid booking — if they don't hold a "foreign guest reception" license (涉外酒店许可证). This is a police registration rule, not discrimination. Hotels must register foreign guests with local Public Security, and without the right paperwork they simply can't process you.

⚠️ The $20 trap
It's common for a budget hotel to accept your booking, take your money, then refuse you at the desk because they can't register a foreign passport. You lose time finding another place and may not get a quick refund. Always confirm the hotel accepts foreigners before you click "book."

How to check if a hotel accepts foreigners

  • Trip.com (English): Most reliable for foreigners. Listings clearly state whether foreign passports are accepted, and the English site filters heavily toward eligible properties.
  • Booking.com: Less consistent — read "Policies" carefully for phrases like "All guests welcome" or "International ID accepted."
  • International chains (Marriott, Hilton, InterContinental, Hyatt, Accor) are always licensed. Safe default for a first trip.
  • When in doubt, message the hotel before booking. Trip.com has built-in chat with auto-translate. A simple "Do you accept foreign passports?" gets you a yes/no.

At the hotel: check-in step-by-step

What you need

  • Your physical passport with the entry stamp. A photo or copy doesn't work — they need the real document. They will take it to a back office for 1-3 minutes to scan into the police system. This is normal and legally required.
  • Your booking confirmation — screenshot it offline. Saves time if there's confusion.
  • The name on the booking must match the passport. "Robert" vs "Robert James Smith" is fine; "Bob" vs your legal name can cause problems.

The conversation (it's short)

Reception staff at foreigner-licensed hotels in tier-1/2 cities usually speak enough English for check-in. Expect this flow:

1
"Hello, checking in."

Hand over your passport(s) to the receptionist.

2
Passport scan

They find your entry stamp and scan/photograph the passport and stamp page.

3
Registration form

You may sign a short paper form (name, nationality, passport number, arrival date) — this is the police registration record.

4
Deposit

They ask for a refundable deposit — see the section below.

5
Room card & info

You get your room card, breakfast info, and elevator directions.

ℹ️ Police registration is automatic
By law, foreign visitors must register their address with police within 24 hours of arrival. Hotels do this for you automatically when they scan your passport — that's the whole reason for the scan. If you stay at an Airbnb or with friends, your host is supposed to register you (less reliable). Keep your hotel check-in slip — it's your proof of registration if anyone asks (extremely rare for tourists, but technically required).

Deposits (押金): what to expect

Virtually all Chinese hotels take a refundable deposit at check-in to cover minibar, room service, or damages. Typical amounts:

  • Budget/business hotels (¥200-500/night): deposit ¥200-500 total.
  • Mid-range (¥500-1000/night): deposit ¥500-1000.
  • Upscale international chains: deposit ¥1000-3000+.

How to pay:

  • Credit card hold (easiest): International chains accept Visa/Mastercard for a pre-authorization. The hold releases 1-2 weeks after check-out.
  • Alipay / WeChat Pay: Most hotels support a temporary hold via app (works with foreign cards linked to Alipay). Auto-refunds to your wallet in 1-3 days.
  • Cash: Possible but increasingly rare. Hand over cash, get a receipt, reclaim at check-out.
⚠️ Always get a deposit receipt
Whether paid by card, app, or cash, ask for a physical or digital receipt for the deposit. You'll need the reference number to claim the refund at check-out.

During your stay

  • Key card power slot: A slot by the door holds your key card — insert it to power lights and AC. Take the card when you leave (AC turns off, this is normal).
  • Drinking water: Free bottled water is standard (usually 2 bottles/day). Tap water is NOT drinkable anywhere in China, including nice hotels. Use the electric kettle or buy bottles.
  • WiFi: Free and fast. International chain hotels usually have open internet. Domestic/budget hotels sometimes don't — you'll want a working eSIM regardless. Password is often printed on the key card sleeve.
  • Toothbrush and slippers are provided even at budget hotels — cultural norm, not a comment on your hygiene.

Check-out

Check-out is usually 12 noon (some budget hotels 11 AM). Late check-out to 2 PM is often free if you ask.

  1. Bring your key card and deposit receipt to the desk.
  2. They call housekeeping — a 2-3 minute wait.
  3. Card holds are released automatically (no action from you, takes days to weeks to clear your statement).
  4. Alipay/WeChat/cash deposits are refunded on the spot.
  5. Ask for a "fapiao" (发票, official invoice) if you need one for business.
💡 Take the business card
Grab a hotel business card from the front desk at check-in. It has the name and address in Chinese. Hand it to taxi drivers when you can't pronounce the name — saves a confusing conversation.

Common issues

"We can't accept foreign guests"

If you booked through Trip.com and they told you foreigners are accepted, call Trip.com's 24/7 English line. They'll either get the hotel to honor the booking or relocate you and refund the difference. Don't argue with the receptionist — the decision isn't theirs.

Deposit not refunded

Card holds take time — wait 1-2 weeks. Alipay/WeChat refunds normally arrive within 5 days; after that, message the hotel (Trip.com can help translate).

Nobody speaks English

Smaller hotels may have zero English. Use Google Translate offline (download the Chinese pack before you arrive) or Trip.com's built-in translation chat. The check-in vocabulary is tiny — passport, booking, deposit, key — and you can do it with hand gestures and screenshots.

Quick checklist before your trip

  1. Book via Trip.com English (or international chain direct), confirm foreigner acceptance.
  2. Screenshot your booking offline.
  3. Bring your physical passport.
  4. Set up Alipay for deposit payment (smoother than cash).
  5. Save the hotel's Chinese name and address in your phone for taxi rides.
  6. Grab the hotel business card at check-in for return trips.

That's the process. It takes 5-10 minutes, and once you've done it once, every subsequent check-in feels identical to anywhere else in the world.
