📱 ESSENTIAL APPS

Must-Have Apps Before You Land in Shanghai (2026 Update)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 10 min read

Arriving in Shanghai for the first time can feel like landing on another planet. Google doesn't work. Cash is politely refused at most shops. The taxi driver stares at you in horror when you say "The Bund." And everyone around you is paying for noodles with a watch.

The good news: with the right 6 or 7 apps, Shanghai becomes one of the most convenient cities on earth. This guide covers exactly which apps to download, how to set them up with your foreign phone number and credit card, and the mistakes to avoid — based on actually installing and using every one of them.

💡 Pro tip
Download these apps before you board the plane. Google Play and the App Store work fine from outside China. Once you land, you'll need a VPN just to read this page — get the VPN first, then come back for the rest.

The big picture: which apps do you actually need?

Here's the brutal truth: you only need a handful. The "top 30 China apps" lists are almost all filler. For a short trip (1–2 weeks), install these before you land:

💬

WeChat

Messaging + payment + mini-programs for everything from DiDi to food delivery. The Swiss Army knife of China.
💳

Alipay

Mobile payment with best foreign card support. Tourist-friendly English setup, links Visa/Mastercard directly.
🚕

DiDi

China's Uber. Works in English, accepts foreign cards. Waving down taxis on the street rarely works in Shanghai.
🗺️

Amap (高德地图)

The only map that actually works in China. Google Maps is blocked. Amap has an English mode and accurate transit info.
🚇

大都会 (Metro)

Shanghai's official metro QR code app. Scan to enter stations — no paper ticket, no line at the machine.
🍜

Meituan / Dianping

Restaurant reviews, food delivery, hotel bookings. Dianping is China's Yelp; Meituan does the deliveries.

For longer stays or more comfort, consider: a translation app (Google Translate offline pack works before you even arrive), a VPN app, and Trip.com for booking trains and hotels in English.

1. WeChat & Alipay — your payment lifelines

Shanghai is functionally cashless. Even street vendors selling 5-kuai jianbing prefer scanning a QR code to taking cash. Most places will accept cash reluctantly; some (especially small restaurants) genuinely cannot make change because they don't carry any. Your foreign credit card works at international hotels and fancy restaurants — and nowhere else.

You need both WeChat and Alipay. WeChat is used for messaging and social features (you'll need it to add guides, shop owners, and anyone you meet). Alipay has smoother international card support and is the safer first choice for payment. They both support foreign Visa and Mastercard as of 2025–2026 policy changes.

Alipay: the easier one for tourists

1
Download Alipay

Available on both App Store and Google Play. Search "Alipay" — the icon is blue with a white "支" character.

2
Register with your foreign phone number

Select your country code, enter your number, receive the SMS code. No Chinese ID or bank account required.

3
Verify your identity

Tap "Me" → "Bank Cards" and follow the prompts. You'll scan your passport. This takes about 2 minutes and is fully automated.

4
Link your Visa/Mastercard

Add an international credit or debit card. A ~3% foreign transaction fee applies (charged by your card, not Alipay) but it's worth it for the convenience.

5
Switch to English

Me (bottom right) → Settings (gear icon) → General → Language → English. The entire app switches over.

✅ Limits updated 2025
Transaction limits for foreign cards were raised to $5,000 per transaction and $50,000 per year. That's more than enough for any tourist.

WeChat: messaging + backup payment

Setup is almost identical to Alipay — download, register with your foreign number, scan passport, link card. The payment interface (WeChat Pay) lives under "Me" → "Services." WeChat Pay is less forgiving than Alipay for foreign card users (some transactions randomly decline), so use Alipay as your primary and WeChat as backup.

⚠️ Watch out
Some banks block Alipay/WeChat transactions as suspicious international activity. Call your bank before you leave and tell them you'll be making charges in China. A 3-minute phone call saves a lot of panic at the noodle shop.

2. DiDi — taxis that actually pick you up

Forget hailing a cab in central Shanghai. The vast majority of drivers only take ride-hailing orders, and many will drive past a waving foreigner rather than deal with the language barrier. DiDi solves this — it has a full English interface, shows the driver your destination on a map, and charges you through the app so no money changes hands.

Setup

  • Download: Search "DiDi" or "DiDi-Rider" in your app store. Note: the international version ("DiDi Rider") is separate from the Chinese "滴滴出行." Either works; the Chinese version has more features.
  • Register: Use your international phone number or email. No Chinese ID needed.
  • Language: Tap the profile icon (bottom right) → Settings → Language → English.
  • Payment: Go to "Payment Methods." You can add an international credit card, or link Alipay/WeChat Pay (recommended — faster and more reliable than card directly).

How to actually use it

1
Type your destination in English

Famous landmarks ("The Bund," "Jing'an Temple," "Shanghai Tower") work directly. For hotels, type the English name. For restaurants, have the Chinese address ready and paste it in.

2
Choose a car type

"Express" (快车) is the default cheap option. "Premium" is nicer sedans for ~50% more. "Taxi" calls an actual metered cab (less common now). You can select multiple types at once.

3
Confirm your pickup point carefully

GPS in Shanghai's dense areas can be off by a building. Long-press the map to drag the pin to exactly where you're standing. The driver will follow the pin, not your description.

4
Wait 1–3 minutes

Drivers accept fast. You'll see the car approach on the map and the license plate. You don't need to call the driver — they can see your destination.

ℹ️ Good to know
The driver may call you to confirm. Don't panic — most call just to hear you confirm you're there. Say "wei" (hello) and stay near the pin. They usually hang up after 3 seconds once they know you're real.

3. Public transport — how to ride the metro like a local

Shanghai has one of the largest and cleanest metro systems in the world. A ride across the entire city costs roughly $1. It's faster than a taxi during rush hour and operates from roughly 5:30 AM to 10:30 PM depending on the line.

Option A: The 大都会 app (official)

Download "Metro 大都会" (the icon is red with a metro train). Register with your phone number, then tap the QR code icon to bring up a code that you scan at the turnstile. You can link Alipay to pay per ride automatically.

Option B: Use Alipay directly (easiest for tourists)

Open Alipay → search "Shanghai Metro" or tap "Transport" on the home screen. You'll get a QR code to scan at the gate. No separate app needed. This works in most major Chinese cities now, not just Shanghai.

💰 Save money
Single-journey paper tickets from the station machines require cash or a Chinese card. Skip the line — use the app.

Buses are more complex (stop announcements in Chinese only) — stick to the metro and DiDi for your first week.

4. Maps — Amap (高德地图), because Google won't work

Google Maps is completely blocked in China without a VPN, and even with a VPN it's missing Chinese data (most small businesses aren't listed, transit info is wrong). Apple Maps works partially but isn't reliable. You need a Chinese map app, and Amap (高德地图) is the best for foreigners.

  • Download: Search "Amap" or "高德地图" — the icon is a paper plane on a blue background.
  • Language: Amap has a built-in English mode. Go to My → Settings → General → Language → English. Most map labels remain in Chinese, but the UI and navigation prompts switch to English.
  • Why not Baidu Maps? Baidu Maps requires a Chinese phone number for registration and is harder for foreigners. Amap is the standard choice for travelers.
💡 Useful trick
Before your trip, save your hotel, key restaurants, and attractions as "Favorites" in Amap while you still have Google access. The Chinese names and addresses are stored in the app — you can show them to taxi drivers or use them in DiDi without typing Chinese.

5. Food: Meituan & Dianping

China is the only country where food delivery is better, faster, and cheaper than cooking. You don't need these apps to survive, but they dramatically improve your trip.

  • Dianping (大众点评) — China's Yelp + TripAdvisor. Restaurant reviews, photos, ratings, opening hours. The best way to find food near you. The app is mostly Chinese but you can search in pinyin (e.g., "xiaolongbao") and the photos tell you everything.
  • Meituan (美团) — Handles the actual food delivery. Search in English or pinyin, show the delivery person your address in Chinese (saved in Amap). Payment via Alipay.

For tourists, we recommend Dianping for discovery and just ordering at restaurants for the experience — delivery is great for late-night hotel snacks.

6. Stay connected: set up before you leave home

Many of the online services you use every day — your email, your usual messaging apps, your home-country maps, your airline and banking apps — don't load reliably on a Chinese network. Sort out your connectivity before you fly, because the websites and app-store pages for these services are often unreachable once you're in China.

  • What to get: Choose 1–2 reputable commercial connectivity services (we have a full comparison here). Free services are unreliable for travel. Expect to pay $5–12/month.
  • Install and test it at home. Make sure you can log in and connect. Test both your phone and laptop.
  • Set it to auto-connect before you get on the plane. The second you connect to airport WiFi in Shanghai, you'll want it running.

A reliable connectivity service lets you keep using your own email, messaging apps, maps, and travel accounts throughout your trip — just as you would in any other country. Without it, many of the apps and services you rely on at home won't load on a Chinese network.

Quick-start checklist

Here's your action list in order. Do these 10 things before departure:

  1. Arrange a reliable connectivity service for your phone and laptop before departure
  2. Download Alipay, register, scan passport, link a Visa/Mastercard
  3. Download WeChat, register, link a card (backup payment)
  4. Call your bank — tell them you're traveling to China
  5. Download DiDi and register with your phone number
  6. Download Amap and switch to English
  7. Download Google Translate and the Chinese offline pack
  8. Buy an eSIM (we recommend Airalo or Nomad — comparison here) or plan to pick up a Chinese SIM at Pudong airport
  9. Save your hotel's Chinese address + name into Amap favorites
  10. Bookmark this guide in case anything goes sideways at the airport

That's it. You'll land, connect to airport WiFi (free at Pudong), launch your connectivity app, open Alipay for a taxi into the city via DiDi, and you're operational. The rest of China runs on the same six apps — learn them once and you can travel anywhere from Shanghai to Chengdu to Kashgar without a problem.
