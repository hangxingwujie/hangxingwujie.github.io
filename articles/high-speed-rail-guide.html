How to Buy China High-Speed Train Tickets as a Foreigner (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 9 min read

China's high-speed rail network is the largest in the world. Trains run at 300 km/h, leave on the minute, and cost a fraction of what a comparable train costs in Europe or Japan. Beijing to Shanghai: 4.5 hours, ¥550-700 ($75-100). Shanghai to Hangzhou: 45 minutes, ¥73 ($10). Chengdu to Xi'an: 3 hours, ¥263 ($37). It's the single best way to see multiple cities in one trip.

The ticketing system is built for Chinese ID card holders, but foreigners have clean options now. This guide covers both the official 12306 English app and the simpler Trip.com alternative.

Option A: Trip.com (recommended for most travelers)

Trip.com is the English-language arm of Ctrip (China's largest OTA). Search train tickets on Trip.com →

1
Open Trip.com app or website → Trains

Enter departure city (e.g., "Shanghai"), destination ("Beijing"), date. The search returns all G-, D-, and C-class trains (G = high-speed 300 km/h, D = 250 km/h, C = intercity).

2
Pick your train and seat class

Second class (二等座): 3+2 seats, ¥550 Beijing-Shanghai. Standard choice. First class (一等座): 2+2 seats, wider, ~¥930 same route. Business class (商务座): 2+1, lie-flat seats, ¥1750. Three seats per row, worth it on 5+ hour rides.

3
Enter passenger info — YOUR PASSPORT, exactly as printed

Trip.com asks for passenger name (match your passport precisely — "ROBERT JAMES SMITH," not "Bob Smith"), passport number, nationality, and date of birth. This is the ticket identity check. If the name doesn't match your passport, you cannot collect the ticket.

4
Pay with international card or Alipay

Visa, Mastercard, and Alipay all work. Your ticket is booked.

💡 Buy early
Tickets go on sale 15 days before departure (sometimes longer for certain routes). Popular routes — Friday afternoon Beijing-Shanghai, holiday weekends, early-morning trains — sell out. Book 5-7 days ahead for weekend travel, 10+ days for holidays.

Option B: 12306 Official English App

The official China Railway app (铁路12306) now has an English version. It's slightly more complicated than Trip.com but gives you direct access to the system — no middleman, slightly faster refunds, and the ability to change tickets directly.

Setting up 12306 as a foreigner

1
Download the app

Search "12306" or "Railway 12306" in your app store. The icon is a blue train on a white background.

2
Switch to English

Tap "Me" (bottom right) → Settings gear → Language → English. The app reloads.

3
Register with your passport

Tap "Register." Select "Passport" as ID type. Enter your passport number, name (exactly as printed), nationality, date of birth, phone number (foreign number works), and email. You'll receive an SMS verification code.

4
Verify your identity (the annoying part)

12306 may require manual identity verification for foreign passports. After registering, check your status under "Me" → "Identity Verification." If it says "not verified," you have two options: (a) go to any train station ticket counter with your passport and ask them to verify you (takes 2 minutes, free), or (b) use Trip.com instead — it handles this step automatically.

⚠️ The 12306 verification problem
Many foreign users can't complete online verification on 12306. The app's facial-recognition step sometimes rejects foreign passport photos. If registration fails after two attempts, don't keep trying — switch to Trip.com. It's not worth the frustration. 12306 is a good tool once verified, but Trip.com gets you the same ticket with zero friction.

Booking on 12306 (once verified)

  1. Tap "Train" → enter cities, date, tap "Query."
  2. Choose a train. Toggle "High-speed only" to filter out slow trains. Each listing shows departure, arrival, duration, and remaining seats.
  3. Select seat class. Add passenger. Pay with Alipay, WeChat, or UnionPay (international cards via Alipay work).

How to collect your ticket at the station

China's high-speed rail stations are massive, modern, and bilingual. Arrive 45-60 minutes before departure for your first trip (you'll be faster later).

1
Find the ticket office or self-service machine

Look for signs saying "Ticket Office / 售票处" or rows of blue self-service ticket machines. At major stations like Shanghai Hongqiao and Beijing South, there's a dedicated "Foreign Passport" or "Manual Service" window at the ticket office — use this if you're unsure.

2
If using Trip.com:

Trip.com sends you a pickup code (E-ticket number) by email and in-app. Take this code and your passport to the ticket counter. The agent prints a physical ticket.

3
If using 12306:

At the self-service machine, tap "Internet Ticket Pickup" → scan your passport on the designated scanner → your bookings appear → print ticket. Some machines don't read foreign passports well — if it fails, go to the manual counter.

ℹ️ E-tickets are mostly rolled out
As of 2025-2026, many high-speed rail stations now support boarding with passport only — no paper ticket needed. When you book on 12306 or Trip.com, check if your route supports e-ticket. At the station, scan your passport at the automated gate instead of inserting a paper ticket. If in doubt, always collect the paper ticket as backup.

Boarding the train

  1. Security check: Like the metro, you put bags through an X-ray and walk through a metal detector. 30 seconds. No liquids confiscated (unlike airports).
  2. Find your gate: Your ticket or app shows the gate number (检票口). Large screens in the waiting hall also list it. Gates open about 15 minutes before departure.
  3. Board: Insert your paper ticket into the gate slot OR scan your passport on the reader. Gate opens. Walk to the platform, find your car number (printed on your ticket, marked on the platform floor in yellow).
  4. Find your seat: Seat numbers are marked above the windows — A (window) / B (middle) / C (aisle) / D (aisle) / F (window) in second class. There's no E in the Chinese seat numbering.

On board

  • Free WiFi on many high-speed trains. Look for the "Train-WiFi" network. Requires a Chinese phone number for the SMS code on some trains — not always foreigner-friendly.
  • Power outlets under the seats (second class) or in the armrest (first/business). Two-pin Chinese sockets + USB.
  • Food: A dining car sells hot meals (¥40-60), instant noodles, snacks, and drinks. A staff member also walks through selling snacks. You can bring your own food. Hot water dispensers for instant noodles are at the end of every car.
  • Clean squat and Western toilets at the end of every car.

Changing or refunding tickets

  • Trip.com: Open "My Bookings" → the train ticket → "Modify" or "Cancel." Refunds go back to your card within 3-15 business days. A small fee applies (typically 5-20% depending on how close to departure).
  • 12306: "My Orders" → select the ticket → "Change" or "Refund." Change is free once per ticket.
  • At the station: At the ticket counter with your passport and paper ticket. Do this at least 30 minutes before departure.

Quick tips

  1. Use Trip.com for your first trip. Switch to 12306 once you're comfortable and have passport-verified it.
  2. Arrive 45-60 minutes early for your first train. Stations are well-signed but enormous.
  3. Book weekend and holiday trains 5-10 days ahead. They sell out.
  4. Your name on the ticket must match your passport exactly.
  5. Keep your paper ticket until you exit the destination station — you need it (or your passport for e-ticket gates) to exit.

Once you've done one high-speed trip in China, you'll wonder why every country hasn't built this. It's faster than flying (no airport commute, no security lines), cheaper than Europe or Japan, and staggeringly efficient. The ticketing system is the only hard part, and Trip.com solves it.
