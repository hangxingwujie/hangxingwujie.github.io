How to Use Dianping & Xiaohongshu in China (No Chinese Required)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Google Maps reviews don't exist in China. TripAdvisor's Chinese listings are sparse and outdated. Yelp never launched here. So how do you find the best dumpling shop in Shanghai, the coolest cafe in Chengdu, or the hiking trail in Hangzhou that tourists never find?

Two apps. Dianping (China's Yelp, but better) and Xiaohongshu (RED — China's Instagram-meets-TripAdvisor). Both are Chinese-only. Both are 100% usable by anyone with eyes and a phone. This guide shows you how to navigate both by photo, find exactly what you want, and save it for when you get there.

⭐

Dianping (大众点评)

For: finding restaurants, cafes, bars, spas. Real user reviews + photo galleries + map search. Local consensus on what's good. Price ranges, opening hours, and popularity rankings. Use when you think "where should I eat tonight?"
📕

Xiaohongshu / RED (小红书)

For: discovering photogenic cafes, photo spots, hiking routes, shopping, itineraries. User-generated content with beautiful photos. Influencer-driven but real locations. Use when you think "what's the coolest place near me that Instagram would love?"

Part 1: Dianping — find the best food, guaranteed

Getting started

Download "大众点评" from your app store (orange icon with a white crown/star). You don't need a full account — the app works for browsing without signup, but to save favorites you'll register with your phone number. It's in Chinese, but here's the map:

  • Bottom bar, left icon (首页): Home — trending restaurants near you.
  • Bottom bar, second icon (美食): Food — the main restaurant browser.
  • Bottom bar, middle icon (➕): Post a review (ignore).
  • Bottom bar, fourth icon (消息): Messages.
  • Bottom bar, right icon (我的): Profile / favorites.

How to find a great restaurant in 30 seconds

1
Tap the Food tab (美食) → the map view opens

The map shows restaurants near your current location as pins. Zoom in on an area like "French Concession" or "near my hotel." Each pin has a star rating and average price.

2
Filter by category — the icons are universal

Scroll down past the map. You'll see colored category chips: 🍜 火锅 (hot pot), 🍢 烧烤 (BBQ), 🍵 咖啡 (coffee), 🍰 甜点 (dessert), 🍣 日料 (Japanese), etc. The emojis make these usable even without reading Chinese. Tap one to filter.

3
Pick a restaurant by its signals

Look for: ⭐ 4.5+ rating + 100+ reviews + ¥¥ (mid-range) = nearly guaranteed great meal. A restaurant with a "#1" badge in a category means it's the top-rated in that area. Restaurants with a "必吃榜" (must-eat list) badge are Dianping's editor-curated picks — nearly foolproof.

4
Inside the restaurant page: scroll the photos

Tap a restaurant → scroll down to the photo gallery. Above it is a section labeled "推荐菜" (recommended dishes) — these are the most-ordered items, ranked by popularity. Each has a photo and a number of people who ordered it. Tap any dish to see close-up photos from real diners. This is how you pick what to order without reading a menu.

💡 The screenshot trick
At the restaurant, show a Dianping photo of the dish you want to your server. Point at the photo, smile. They'll know exactly what you mean. This works 100% of the time and is completely normal — locals do it too.

More than food: what else Dianping does

  • Hair Salons / Spas / Massage: Categories under 丽人 (lifestyle). Filter by ⭐ rating + number of reviews.
  • Attractions & Parks: Under 景点. Shows opening hours, ticket prices (in ¥), and visitor reviews.
  • Hotels (for price comparison): Dianping lists hotels with real traveler photos — useful to cross-check against Trip.com photos.
  • Book a table: Some restaurants have "订座" (book a seat). Tap it, choose date/time, arrive. Useful for popular places on weekends.

Part 2: Xiaohongshu (RED) — discover hidden gems

Xiaohongshu (小红书, literally "Little Red Book") is where Chinese locals post their real experiences — the photogenic cafe with a secret rooftop, the hiking trail nobody writes about in English, the vintage shop tucked in a lane. It's visual-first: every post has photos and a location tag. You don't need to read Chinese to use it; you need to recognize what looks good.

How to use it as a foreigner

1
Download RED / 小红书 (red icon with white "RED")

Register with your phone number. Set your interests — tap categories that look appealing (photos tell you everything).

2
Search in English — it works now

The search bar at the top accepts English queries. Try: "Shanghai brunch," "Beijing hidden bar," "Chengdu hotpot," "Hangzhou hiking." Results are in Chinese, but the cover photos tell you whether it's what you're looking for.

3
Tap any post → scroll the photos → tap the location tag

Every post has a pinned location at the top. Tap the location name, and you go to a page with: the address (in Chinese — copy it to Amap), a map pin, related posts from the same place, and real visitor photos.

4
Save posts to collections for your trip

The bookmark icon (bottom-right of every post) saves it to a collection. Create collections like "Shanghai food" or "Beijing sights" and review them when you're at the destination.

🔍 Search like this
Xiaohongshu's search understands English city names + English keywords. The best searches are: "[city] [thing]" — "Shanghai cocktail bar," "Beijing vintage shopping," "Guangzhou dim sum." The results are all real posts by locals who've been there.

Why Xiaohongshu beats Google for China travel

  • Real photos by real people: No stock photography. If the lighting is bad, you'll see it. If the cafe is beautiful, you'll know.
  • Updated constantly: A restaurant that opened last week has posts. A cafe that closed last month shows "permanently closed" warnings.
  • Price transparency: Posts often include the bill (screenshot of the receipt). You know exactly what you'll pay.
  • "How to get there" instructions: Many posts include the nearest metro exit, walking directions, or tips like "behind the yellow building."
  • No language barrier for photos: A beautiful plate of dumplings doesn't need a caption.

Quick workflow: from discovery to dining

  1. At the hotel, afternoon: Open Dianping → map view → find a 4.5⭐ restaurant near you → tap it → scroll "recommended dishes" → screenshot 3 dishes you want to try.
  2. Optional: Open Xiaohongshu → search "[city] [restaurant name]" → see what the place actually looks like inside. A beautiful Dianping page sometimes hides ugly reality — Xiaohongshu reveals it.
  3. Save the address: On the Dianping page, tap the address → "Copy." Paste it into Amap to see the route. Or show it to a DiDi driver.
  4. At the restaurant: Show the waiter your Dianping screenshots of the recommended dishes. Point, smile. "Zhege" (this one). Order placed.

Neither app requires Chinese reading ability. Both reward people who browse by photo. After two meals discovered through Dianping, you'll wonder how you ever traveled without it.
