🍜 FOOD & DELIVERY

How to Order Food Delivery in China: Meituan & Ele.me for Foreigners (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

It's 10 PM. You're jet-lagged in your hotel. The restaurant downstairs closed hours ago. In most countries, you'd open Uber Eats and wait 45 minutes for a cold burger. In China, you open Meituan, scroll through 200 restaurants within 3 km, pick a steaming bowl of Lanzhou beef noodles for ¥23 ($3.20), and a guy on an electric scooter delivers it to your hotel lobby in 22 minutes. The delivery fee is ¥3 ($0.40).

Food delivery in China is absurdly good, absurdly cheap, and — once you get past the Chinese-only interface — usable by anyone with a phone and Alipay. This guide shows you how to do it tonight.

ℹ️ Both apps are Chinese-only — here's the workaround
Neither Meituan nor Ele.me has an English interface. But you don't need to read menus — you use Google Translate's camera mode pointed at your phone screen. We'll show you exactly how.

Step 1 — Choose your app

🟠

Meituan (美团)

The dominant player. More restaurants, slightly harder interface (busy homepage). Yellow app icon. Used by 75%+ of food delivery in China.
🔵

Ele.me (饿了么)

Alibaba's delivery app. Cleaner interface, slightly fewer restaurants. Blue icon. Owned by the same company as Alipay, so integration is seamless.

Pick one. Don't install both. The apps are nearly identical in function and restaurant coverage. Meituan has more options; Ele.me has a cleaner layout. For a first-time user, Ele.me is slightly easier to navigate because the menu categories are more visual. For maximum restaurant choice, Meituan wins.

💡 Save space
You don't need either app installed. Open Alipay, search "Meituan" or "Ele.me" in the search bar, and tap the mini-program. It's the full app functionality without downloading anything. The mini-program also auto-fills your Alipay delivery address.

Step 2 — Set your delivery address

This is the single most important step. If the address is wrong, your noodles go to someone else.

1
In Meituan/Ele.me mini-program, tap the address bar at the top

It usually says "请添加收货地址" (add delivery address). This opens a map.

2
Drop a pin on your hotel or current location

The map is a standard map — you don't need to read Chinese to use it. Pinch-zoom, drop a pin on your current building.

3
Add your room number and full name in the detail field

In the text field below the map, type your room number (e.g., "Room 1205, 12F"), your first name, and "Front Desk" in English. The delivery person reads this. Chinese characters are helpful but not required — drivers in big cities are used to English room numbers.

4
Better: paste your hotel's full Chinese address

From your Trip.com booking or hotel business card, copy the Chinese address and paste it into the detail field. Then add your room number after it. This is the most reliable option.

⚠️ The hotel lobby problem
Most delivery drivers can't enter hotel elevators (they need a room key). Your food will be left at the front desk, or the driver will call you. If you get a call from an unknown Chinese number, it's the driver. They'll say "waimai" (why-my — delivery) or "da le" (dah luh — arrived). Say "lobby — xiexie" and go downstairs. If a hotel has a delivery robot, it'll come to your door — no interaction needed.

Step 3 — Browse and order

1
Tap the food category (外卖 / 美食)

This opens the restaurant grid. Each card shows: cover photo of food, restaurant name, rating in stars, delivery time in minutes, delivery fee.

2
Pick by photo — it works perfectly

Scroll the grid. Tap any card where the food photo looks good. The photos are real — not stock photography. If a restaurant has dishes that look appetizing, they'll taste like what they show.

3
Inside the restaurant, scroll the menu and tap dishes

Every dish has a photo. Tap "+" to add it to your basket. If you need to read the name: open Google Translate camera, point it at your screen showing the Chinese menu, and the dish name overlays in English in real time.

4
Watch three signals when picking a restaurant

⭐ 4.5+ stars with 500+ reviews: reliably good. ⏱️ 25-35 min delivery time: normal (shorter = closer, longer = further). 📦 ¥0-5 delivery fee: most deliver nearly free within 3 km.

Step 4 — Pay and track

1
Tap the basket icon, review your order, tap "Submit" (提交订单)

You'll see the total: food + delivery fee + packaging fee (a ¥1-3 box-fee surcharge on every item — normal and non-negotiable). The total for a typical meal: ¥25-50 ($3.50-$7).

2
Pay through Alipay/WeChat

Select Alipay as the payment method. Your linked foreign card works. Confirm the payment.

3
Watch the live GPS tracking

The app shows a map with a tiny scooter icon moving towards you. You'll see "rider accepted order" → "restaurant is preparing" → "rider has picked up" → "rider is approaching." It's weirdly addictive. Delivery usually takes 25-40 minutes.

Pro tips for smooth delivery

  • Order 30-45 minutes before you want to eat. Food arrives hot because riders are fast and the containers are sealed.
  • There's always a packaging fee (¥1-3 per item). This is standard — it covers the sealed container and disposable chopsticks.
  • "No spicy" option: Look for "口味选择" (taste options) under the dish. "不辣" means no spice. Not every dish has this option.
  • Cancelling: Tap "取消订单" (cancel order) before the restaurant starts cooking. After cooking starts, cancellations may incur a fee.
  • No tipping: Delivery is prepaid. Tipping is not expected. A ¥1-2 tip in the app is possible but completely optional.
  • Late-night ordering: Delivery coverage drops after 10 PM in smaller cities but is 24/7 in Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen.

Common problems

The driver can't find me

Open the app → the order screen has a "contact rider" button (联系骑手) — tap it, then tap the phone icon that appears. It calls the driver. The conversation goes: they say "waimai" (delivery), you say "lobby" or "front desk." Hang up. They'll find you.

Something is wrong with my order

Tap the order → "customer service" (客服) or "refund" (退款). The in-app support is fast and usually auto-refunds for obvious problems (wrong item, damaged container, late delivery). No need to argue — the system is automated and consumer-friendly.

The food address is in Chinese and I can't read it

Add your hotel's Chinese name + address from Trip.com. Then add "Room XXX, [Your Name]" in English. The driver navigates to the building via GPS; your hotel name helps them confirm; your room number tells them where to leave it.

After your first delivery, you'll realize this is the secret superpower of Chinese cities — hot, fresh, cheap food from hundreds of restaurants, in your hands in 25 minutes, without leaving your room or speaking a word.
