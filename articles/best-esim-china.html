📶 eSIM & SIM

Best eSIM for China in 2026: Airalo vs Nomad vs Saily vs Holafly (Tested)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 10 min read

Walk off any long-haul flight into Shanghai Pudong or Beijing Capital, and the first thing you'll see is a line of exhausted travelers queued at the China Mobile counter. They'll wait 20-40 minutes, hand over a passport for real-name registration, and pay roughly $20+ for a week of data. You can skip all of that.

An eSIM installs before you board, activates the second you land, and — the key point for China — most eSIMs route data through Hong Kong or Singapore, so the email and apps you use at home load normally without any extra setup.

⚠️ Buy before you fly
Install the eSIM while you're still in your home country. Once you land in mainland China, the websites and app stores for these services are not reachable on a Chinese network.

Deep dive: each provider tested

🥇 Saily — easiest one-app setup

Starts at $4.49 / 1GB / 7 days

Saily is built by the team behind Nord Security. When you activate a Saily eSIM in China, data routes through offshore gateways and most apps you use at home load normally without any extra configuration. It also includes tracker and ad blocking.

  • Pros: Dual network (China Unicom + China Mobile) — better signal in smaller cities; one-tap setup; polished app.
  • Cons: Slightly more than Nomad on 5GB+ plans; unlimited plans are the priciest in this group.

Best for: First-time visitors who want the simplest arrival.

🚄 Nomad — best for train travel

Starts at $4 / 1GB / 7 days

Nomad's standout feature is tunnel reconnection. On a Beijing-Shanghai high-speed train you pass through dozens of tunnels; other eSIMs drop for 10-30 seconds each time, but Nomad reconnects in 3-5 seconds. It also has the cheapest 5GB and 10GB plans.

  • Pros: Best value above 5GB, fastest reconnection on trains, APAC regional plan covers China plus Japan/Korea/SE Asia.
  • Cons: No bundled extras; app is less polished.

Best for: Multi-city itineraries and value seekers.

💰 Airalo — best for super-short stays

Starts at $4 / 1GB / 3 days

Airalo is the name most travelers already know. The 1GB plan is the lowest-friction way to get connected for a 2-3 day layover in Shanghai or Beijing.

  • Pros: Polished app, lowest entry price, supports 200+ countries worldwide.
  • Cons: More expensive than Nomad above 5GB; slower tunnel reconnection.

Best for: Short layovers or anyone who already uses Airalo in other countries.

♾️ Holafly — best for unlimited data

Starts at $3.90 / day (unlimited)

Holafly is the only one here with true unlimited data and no cap — though hotspot tethering is limited to 500MB/day. If you video-call home, stream music, or use your phone as a laptop hotspot, it's the most predictable bill.

  • Pros: Truly unlimited; 24/7 real-human chat support; no app needed (install via email QR code); refundable up to 6 months if unused.
  • Cons: Most expensive of the four for the unlimited plans; hotspot cap makes group/laptop use awkward.

Best for: Heavy data users, streamers, and business travelers.

How much data do you actually need?

For reference, a typical week in China with normal usage (maps, messaging, some browsing, a few video calls) uses 1.5-3GB. If you're streaming or posting photos/videos to social media daily, plan for 5-8GB per week.

How to install your eSIM (5 minutes)

1
Buy the plan before you leave home

Use the provider's website or app (iOS / Android). Pay by credit card or PayPal.

2
Check phone compatibility

You need an iPhone XS or newer, Samsung Galaxy S20 or newer, Google Pixel 4 or newer — basically any eSIM-enabled phone from the last 5 years. All providers have a compatibility checker on their site.

3
Receive the QR code by email

It arrives instantly after purchase. Don't scan it yet — wait until step 5.

4
On your phone, go to Settings → Cellular/Mobile → Add eSIM

On iPhone: Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → "Use QR Code." On Android: Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs → "Download a SIM instead?"

5
Scan the QR code at home before departure

The eSIM installs but stays inactive. You'll activate it when you land. Do this on home WiFi — the installation needs a few KB of data.

6
On arrival: turn on Data Roaming and enable the eSIM

Settings → select the new eSIM → toggle "Data Roaming" ON. Select this eSIM as your data line (keep your home SIM active for calls/texts on WiFi calling). It connects within 2-5 minutes.

ℹ️ Keep your home SIM
eSIMs support dual-SIM on modern phones. Keep your physical home SIM in the phone for receiving 2FA codes and banking SMS from home. Set the China eSIM as "data only" and your home SIM as "voice/SMS only" in your phone settings.

eSIM vs. physical SIM card at the airport

You can still buy a physical SIM from China Mobile/Unicom counters at Pudong and Capital airports. Here's the trade-off:

The only reason to get a physical Chinese SIM is if you need a local Chinese phone number for local bookings (some train ticket and food delivery apps require it). For a standard tourist trip, an eSIM is strictly better — it's cheaper, faster to set up, keeps your home number active, and makes your usual apps work on arrival.

⚠️ One more thing
Most China eSIMs do not include a local phone number — they're data-only. You won't be able to make or receive standard voice calls/SMS on the Chinese number. You don't need one: use WhatsApp/WeChat/iMessage/FaceTime over data, or keep your home SIM active for SMS 2FA codes.

Final recommendation

For most travelers, buy Saily (5GB / 30 days, ~$16) — the simplest setup, good coverage, and the built-in security tools are useful on public WiFi.

If you're visiting multiple cities via train, get Nomad (5GB / 30 days, ~$10) — saves $6 and handles tunnels far better.

If you're only laying over for 2-3 days, get Airalo (1GB / 3 days, $4) — the cheapest way to get connected.

If you work remotely or stream a lot, get Holafly (unlimited, from $27/week) — predictable pricing and you can't run out of data mid-video call.

Buy it the day before you fly, install it at home, and activate it on landing. You'll be looking up your hotel on the map while everyone else is still queuing at the SIM counter.
