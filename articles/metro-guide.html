🚇 TRANSPORT

How to Use the Metro in China: Tickets, QR Codes, Apps & Route Maps (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Chinese metros are among the cleanest, cheapest, and most extensive urban transit systems in the world. A ride across an entire city — Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, Chengdu — costs roughly ¥3-9 ($0.40-1.30). The signage is bilingual. The trains are air-conditioned. And the only hard part is that first time you stand in front of the ticket machine realizing none of your payment methods work.

This guide gets you from the station entrance to your exit without ever touching cash or waiting in the ticket line.

The easiest method: Alipay transport QR code

Since 2024-2025, Alipay has integrated city metro QR codes directly into the app. You don't need a separate metro app — it lives inside Alipay. This works in 300+ cities across China, including all the ones you'll visit.

1
Open Alipay, tap "Transport" on the home screen

If it's not on your home screen, search "transport" or "出行" in the app's search bar. If it's your first time, Alipay may ask you to enable a city-specific transit card — tap yes, it's instant and free.

2
A QR code appears — point it at the gate scanner

At the metro entrance, every turnstile has a small scanning window (usually marked with a phone icon or QR symbol). Hold your phone screen about 5 cm from the scanner. The gate opens in under a second.

3
At your exit station, scan again

Same process — open Transport, scan the QR code, gate opens. Fare is auto-calculated and deducted from your linked Alipay card.

ℹ️ Works with your foreign card
The Alipay transport QR pulls from your Alipay balance (which is fed by your linked Visa/Mastercard). No separate setup needed. If you've set up Alipay, you can ride the metro.

If Alipay isn't working: what else works

📱

City metro app

Each city has its own: "大都会" for Shanghai, "亿通行" for Beijing, "广州地铁" for Guangzhou. All have basic English, all support Alipay-linked payment. Download if you plan to ride a lot in one city.
🎫

Single-journey ticket machine

The fallback. Machines have English, take ¥10/¥20 bills and ¥1 coins (no cards, no Alipay). Tap your destination station on the touch map, insert cash, get a plastic token. Tap token at gate, insert token at exit.
🚌

Transport card (实体卡)

Buy a physical card at the service counter (¥20 deposit). Works on metro + bus. Not worth it for short trips, but if you're riding daily for 2+ weeks, it saves the QR scanning step.
💳

UnionPay / Foreign contactless

Some gates in Shanghai, Beijing, and Guangzhou accept contactless Visa/Mastercard directly — tap in, tap out. Still rolling out. Don't rely on this as your primary plan.
⚠️ The ticket machine trap
Ticket machines take cash only (small bills or coins). No credit cards, no Alipay/WeChat, no foreign cards. If you didn't bring yuan bills and haven't set up Alipay, you're stuck standing at the machine watching other people tap their phones and glide through. Set up Alipay before you travel.

How to read a Chinese metro map

Every station has a bilingual (Chinese/English) route map above every platform door and throughout the station. They're easier to read than they look:

  • Each line has its own color. Shanghai Line 2 is green. Beijing Line 1 is red. The color is consistent across all maps, signs, and trains.
  • Stations are dots with English names. "Jing'an Temple," "Nanjing Road East," "Beijing South Railway Station."
  • Transfer stations have multiple colored dots. A station where Line 1 (red) meets Line 2 (green) shows both colors. Follow the color of the line you're switching to.
  • The direction is marked by the terminal station name. Signs say "To Disney Resort" or "To East Xujing" — not north/south/east/west. The terminal station is always named in both Chinese and English on platform signs.

Navigate with Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps

For route planning, open Amap and enter your destination in English. Switch the route mode to Transit (公交换乘). Amap tells you:

  • Which line to take and in which direction
  • Where to transfer
  • How many stops until your transfer or destination
  • Which exit to use (exits are numbered A/B/C/D at Chinese stations — using the right one saves walking 500 meters in the wrong direction)
  • Real-time train arrival times
💡 The exit number matters
Amap tells you to "take Exit 2." Chinese metro stations can have 6-10 exits spread across several blocks. Taking the wrong exit can add 10+ minutes of walking. Follow the numbered exit signs in the station — they're clearly marked on the walls.

Security check (yes, every time)

Every metro station has a security checkpoint at every entrance. You put your bag on a conveyor belt and walk through a metal detector. This is universal, not tourist-specific — everyone does it. It takes 10-20 seconds. No, you don't need to take off your shoes or belt. Yes, even a bottle of water sometimes gets a second glance from a handheld scanner — just let them check it, it's routine.

Which cities have great metros?

🔵

Shanghai

World's largest by route length. 19 lines. Covers everywhere a tourist would go. Signage in English throughout. The Maglev to Pudong Airport is connected. ¥3-9 per ride.
🔴

Beijing

27 lines. Connects all major sites including the Great Wall (via Line 8 + bus). Airport Express line from Capital Airport to Dongzhimen (¥25). ¥3-9 per ride.
🟢

Guangzhou / Shenzhen

Guangzhou's 16 lines connect to Foshan. Shenzhen has 16 lines and border crossings to Hong Kong. Both clean, modern, fully bilingual. ¥2-10 per ride.
🟠

Chengdu / Hangzhou / Xi'an

All have 10+ lines now. Chengdu Line 1 to the Panda Base. Hangzhou to West Lake. Xi'an to the Terracotta Warriors. English signage is good but less comprehensive — Amap is essential.

Buses: the harder sibling

Buses are cheaper (¥1-2) and go everywhere metros don't, but they're harder for non-Chinese-readers:

  • Stop announcements are usually Chinese-only (some cities now have English)
  • Route numbers are clear but route maps at bus stops are not always bilingual
  • You scan Alipay's transport QR when boarding (and sometimes when exiting — varies by city)
  • Amap handles bus planning well — it tells you the bus number, when it arrives, and how many stops to ride

For a first trip, stick to metros and DiDi. Buses are for your second visit when you're feeling brave.

Quick checklist

  1. Set up Alipay before you fly — the transport QR is your metro pass
  2. Install Amap for route planning, exit numbers, and real-time arrivals
  3. Bring a few ¥10/¥20 bills as emergency backup for ticket machines
  4. Remember which exit Amap said to take — it matters
  5. Security check is fast and normal — don't stress about it

A metro ride in China costs less than a dollar and gets you anywhere in the city faster than a taxi during peak hours. Once Alipay is set up, you just walk up to the gate, scan, and go — it's the closest thing to teleportation in a Chinese city.
