💳 PAYMENT

How to Set Up Alipay for Foreigners in 2026 (Step-by-Step)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Let's get this out of the way first: you will not survive a week in China as a cash-only tourist. Cash still works at international hotels, big supermarkets, and major tourist sites, but the noodle shop down the lane, the shared bike, the subway vending machine, the taxi driver — they all want a phone.

Alipay is the easiest payment app for foreigners. It supports international Visa and Mastercard, has a full English interface, and takes about 10 minutes to set up from your home country. This guide walks you through every screen, every setting, and every error you might hit.

✅ 2025 update
Transaction limits for foreign cards were raised to $5,000 per transaction and $50,000 per year. That covers essentially any tourist spend. The older $1,000/$10,000 limits no longer apply.

Before you start

  • Your passport (the physical document, not a photocopy) — you'll scan the photo page.
  • A foreign Visa, Mastercard, JCB, or Diners Club card (debit or credit). American Express works but is less widely accepted at merchants.
  • Your regular phone number — no Chinese SIM required.
  • About 10 minutes, ideally at home before you fly, on a stable WiFi connection.
⚠️ Call your bank first
Tell your card issuer you'll be making charges in China. A 3-minute phone call now prevents Alipay transactions from being flagged as fraud and declined at the dumpling shop.

Step 1 — Download Alipay

Alipay is available worldwide on the Apple App Store and Google Play. Search for "Alipay" and look for the blue icon with the white Chinese character "支" (developer: Alipay (Hangzhou) Technology Co. Ltd). Do not download AlipayHK or any regional version.

Step 2 — Register with your foreign phone number

1
Open Alipay and tap "Sign up"

Tap the country code (defaults to +86) and select your home country.

2
Enter your mobile number, then Next

Use your normal number. You'll receive a 6-digit SMS code.

3
Enter the verification code

Arrives in 10-30 seconds. If it doesn't come, tap "Resend."

4
Set a login password

6-digit numeric code, or set up Face ID/fingerprint (recommended — faster for payments).

Step 3 — Switch to English

If the app opens in Chinese:

  1. Tap the Me tab (bottom right, 我的 in Chinese)
  2. Tap the Settings gear icon (top right)
  3. Tap General (通用) → Language (语言)
  4. Select English and confirm. The app reloads in English.

Step 4 — Verify your identity with your passport

1
Me → tap your profile/avatar

Opens your account info.

2
Tap "Identity Verification"

Required to link cards and make payments.

3
Select "Passport"

There's a specific option for foreign passports — do not select Chinese ID.

4
Scan your passport photo page

Make sure the MRZ code (the two chevron lines at the bottom) is fully visible, well-lit, and flat.

5
Take a selfie when prompted

Remove glasses/hats, look straight at the camera. Alipay matches this to your passport photo.

6
Wait for verification

Most tourists are verified within 2 minutes. Occasionally it can take a few hours.

Step 5 — Link your international card

1
Me → Bank Cards → Add Card (+)

You can scan the card with your camera instead of typing.

2
Enter card details

Card number, expiry, CVV, and billing name exactly as printed on the card.

3
Complete 3D Secure

Your bank texts or pushes an OTP. Enter it to authorize the link.

💡 Link two cards if you can
Add a Visa and a Mastercard from different issuers. One of them will occasionally decline (some banks are stricter with China charges). A backup prevents checkout disasters.

Step 6 — How to pay in shops (two methods)

Method A: You scan the merchant's QR code (most common)

  1. Tap Scan (top-left on the home screen)
  2. Point your camera at the printed QR code at the cashier
  3. Enter the amount if prompted (small shops don't pre-fill it)
  4. Tap Pay, authenticate with Face ID/fingerprint
  5. Show the success screen to the cashier

Method B: The cashier scans your code

  1. Tap Pay (big button at the top of the home screen)
  2. A QR code + barcode appears
  3. The cashier scans it with a handheld scanner
  4. Done — no confirmation needed
ℹ️ About the ~3% fee
Alipay itself charges you nothing. The fee you see on your card statement is your bank's standard foreign transaction fee, same as using your card in any other country. Travel rewards cards often waive this.

Common problems and fixes

"Verification failed" while scanning passport

Ensure the MRZ code at the bottom is fully visible and not shadowed. Try a darker background. If it keeps failing, you can type the passport number manually.

"Card declined" at payment

  • Your bank blocked the charge — call them to confirm China travel
  • Try your second linked card
  • Try paying via WeChat Pay as backup (same setup process)

The QR opens a Chinese page I can't read

This happens with non-standard merchant QRs. Ask the cashier to scan your Pay code instead (Method B) — that always works.

App is stuck in Chinese

Kill the app completely and reopen. If it persists, update Alipay to the latest version.

Bonus: Alipay features tourists love

  • Transport code (出行): Generates a metro/bus QR code for Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou and 300+ other cities — no separate metro app needed.
  • Trip.com mini-program: Book trains, flights, and hotels in English without leaving Alipay.
  • DiDi mini-program: Hail a taxi without downloading the DiDi app (the full app is better, but this works in a pinch).
  • Currency converter: Built into the payment screen so you see the cost in your home currency before you tap Pay.
📝 Next steps
Once Alipay is working, set up WeChat Pay as a backup (same process, slightly less friendly UI) and arrange reliable internet access before you fly so everything works the moment you land.
