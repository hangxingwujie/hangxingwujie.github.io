Using DiDi in China: Full English Guide for Foreigners (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 8 min read

Standing on a Shanghai sidewalk waving your arm at passing taxis is a good way to learn that in China, nobody hails cabs on the street anymore. Drivers are all on DiDi — China's version of Uber — and they only take app orders. The good news: DiDi works entirely in English, you can pay with a foreign credit card or Alipay, and a ride across central Shanghai costs roughly the same as a sandwich in most Western cities.

This guide covers downloading, registering, setting up English, paying, calling a ride, choosing car types, and handling the awkward moments (like when the driver calls you and you don't speak Chinese).

Step 1 — Download and register

1
Download the right app

Search your app store for "DiDi" or "DiDi-Rider" or "滴滴出行". The international version ("DiDi Rider") works outside China; the Chinese version (滴滴出行) has more features inside China. You can use either, but the Chinese version connects to local drivers faster. Both have English interfaces.

2
Register with your phone number or email

Use your international number. Select your country code from the dropdown, enter your number, receive the SMS code. No Chinese ID or bank account needed. Email registration also works on the international version.

3
Switch to English immediately

Bottom-right profile icon → Settings gear → Language → select English. The app reloads. From here everything is in English.

Step 2 — Set up payment

DiDi supports 9 payment methods for foreigners. Go to Wallet → Payment Methods and add one of these:

  • Alipay or WeChat Pay (recommended): Link your Alipay account (which you've already set up — see our Alipay guide). Payments route through Alipay and your linked foreign card. This is the smoothest option because you've already verified your identity on Alipay.
  • International credit card directly: Visa, Mastercard, and JCB work when added directly to DiDi. Some banks block this more aggressively than Alipay charges. If your card gets declined, switch to paying through Alipay.
  • Cash: You can select "cash" and pay the driver directly in yuan. This only works if you have physical cash. Many drivers don't carry change. Avoid this unless it's an emergency and Alipay is completely broken.
💡 Just use Alipay
Link DiDi to your Alipay account. It's one tap, it always works, and the charge appears on your card statement the same way as any Alipay transaction. No separate payment setup, no declined cards, no cash hassle.

Step 3 — Call a ride

1
Type your destination in English

Landmarks work directly — type "The Bund," "Jing'an Temple," "Shanghai Tower," "Beijing Capital Airport," etc. For hotels, type the English name from your booking. For restaurants and addresses, it's hit or miss — see "typing addresses" below.

2
Confirm your pickup pin

This is the most important step. The GPS pin for your current location can be off by 20-50 meters in dense city areas. Long-press the map and drag the pin to exactly where you're standing. The driver navigates to the pin, not your description. If you're in front of a hotel, drag the pin to the hotel entrance. If you're on a corner, drag it to the exact corner.

3
Choose a car type

Tap the car-type selector below the destination. See the comparison below. You can select multiple types at once — the app picks whichever arrives fastest.

4
Confirm the fare

The app shows an estimated price range. Tap "Confirm Request." The actual fare is almost always within this range unless there's extreme traffic or a route deviation.

5
Wait 1-3 minutes for a driver to accept

Most rides are accepted within a minute in central areas. You'll see the driver's name, license plate, car model and color, and estimated arrival time on a live map. Walk to the pickup point when you see the car approaching.

Car types explained

🚗

Express (快车)

Standard ride. The default choice. Toyota Corolla / VW Lavida level of car. Clean, air-conditioned, cheapest option. ¥15-30 for most inner-city trips.
🚙

Premium (优享/专车)

Nicer sedan — Toyota Camry, VW Passat or similar. More legroom, drivers in suits, bottled water. Roughly 50% more than Express. Good for airport runs or if you're carrying work stuff.
🚐

6-Seater (六座商务)

Buick GL8 or similar minivan. For groups, families, or anyone with 3+ suitcases. Slightly more than Premium. Airport runs with luggage are the main use case.
🚖

Taxi (出租车)

An actual metered taxi hailed through the app. Same price as a street taxi but you don't need to flag it. The driver may prefer cash — have some yuan ready or confirm Alipay works.

Typing addresses that DiDi can't find

English search in DiDi is good for famous places but bad for small restaurants, residential buildings, and obscure addresses. Solutions:

  • Copy-paste the Chinese address from Dianping, your hotel booking, or Baidu Maps into the destination field. Chinese text works perfectly.
  • Drop a pin manually: Long-press anywhere on the map to drop a pin. Use this when you're looking at the map and can see where you want to go.
  • Search in Amap first, then share to DiDi: Open Amap, find the place (Amap's English search is passable), tap Share → DiDi. The destination transfers over with the exact Chinese name and coordinates.

When the driver calls you (and you don't speak Chinese)

Drivers call to confirm your pickup location — usually when GPS pin ambiguity makes them unsure. They speak zero English. Here's what happens and what to do:

  1. Your phone rings from a Chinese number. Answer it.
  2. The driver says something in Chinese. It's almost always "Where are you?" (你在哪 — nǐ zài nǎ) or "I've arrived" (我到了 — wǒ dào le).
  3. Don't try to explain. Instead, look at the DiDi app — your live position and the driver's position are on the map. If the driver isn't at your pin, use the in-app chat (tap the chat icon) and send the pre-written message "I'm at the pickup point" (most DiDi chat interfaces have canned phrases in Chinese that you can send with one tap).
  4. If the driver hangs up (common — they're just confirming someone is there), stand exactly on the pin and wait.
  5. If the driver cancels, the app assigns a new one automatically. This happens occasionally and isn't a big deal.
ℹ️ The driver is not angry at you
The call sounds urgent to Western ears — Chinese phone conversations are often direct and loud. The driver is just trying to find you quickly. They're not upset. Smile, stand on the pin, and the problem usually resolves itself.

During the ride

  • GPS tracking: You can watch your route on the DiDi map. If the driver takes an unexpected turn, it's almost always traffic avoidance, not a scam. DiDi fares are calculated by distance + time, so route changes don't wildly change the price.
  • Tolls: If the route includes a toll road or airport expressway, the toll is added to your fare automatically — the driver doesn't ask for cash at the toll booth.
  • Silent rides: Chinese taxi and DiDi culture is quiet. Drivers generally don't make small talk. This is normal, not rudeness.

Payment and rating

  • Payment is automatic through Alipay/WeChat or your card at the end of the ride.
  • You'll be prompted to rate the driver. 5 stars = normal. Lower ratings are reserved for genuinely bad experiences (dangerous driving, refusal to follow the route, etc.). Giving 4 stars because the driver didn't smile is considered unfair.
  • You can tip through the app (¥1-10 amounts). Tipping is never expected and most locals never do it. but the feature exists.

Common problems

The driver cancels

This happens. Drivers cancel for many reasons (your pickup is in a congested area, they got a better fare, they're ending their shift). The app auto-reassigns. Wait 30 seconds. If it happens 3+ times in a row, your pickup point is probably in a bad location for drivers — walk 50 meters to a clearer corner or a hotel entrance and re-request.

The fare is higher than the estimate

Minor discrepancy: traffic or a slightly different route. Normal.

Major discrepancy (2x+): open a dispute in the app under "My Trips." DiDi support is responsive in English.

The driver can't find me

Open the in-app chat (speech-bubble icon), tap the quick-send message for "I'm at the pickup point," or use the "Share my location" button that sends your real-time GPS to the driver. This solves 80% of "can't find" problems.

I left something in the car

Open "My Trips" → the specific ride → "Lost & Found" → contact the driver through the app. Rate of recovery is surprisingly high — DiDi takes lost items seriously and drivers are incentivized to return them.

Airport-specific tips

  • At Pudong (Shanghai) and Daxing (Beijing), look for signs for "Online Car-Hailing / Ride-Hailing" — that's the DiDi pickup zone. If you wait in the regular taxi queue a DiDi driver can't reach you.
  • At Capital (Beijing), DiDi pickup is in B2 of the parking garage. Follow "Ride-Hailing" signs.
  • Airport rides have a small surcharge (¥5-10). It's added automatically.

Alternatives to DiDi

DiDi is the standard, but not the only option:

  • Alipay mini-program DiDi: Hail a DiDi without the DiDi app. Open Alipay → search "DiDi" or tap the Transport icon. Slightly fewer features but works for basic rides. Good backup if DiDi app isn't working.
  • Meituan taxi: Meituan (the food delivery app) has a ride-hailing feature. Chinese-only interface, so harder for non-readers.
  • Official taxi rank: Walk to the marked taxi stand. Metered, no app, cash or Alipay. Works for short rides in areas with good taxi flow. Hard to find taxis outside central areas.

For 99% of tourist travel in Chinese cities, DiDi (paid through Alipay) is the answer. It works in English, it's cheap, it's safe, and once you've done it twice it feels as natural as Uber in any city.
