Arriving in China: Step-by-Step Airport Guide (2026)

CT
City Tech Team
Updated July 24, 2026 · 9 min read

Your plane touches down. The seatbelt sign turns off. Everyone stands up at once. You're excited, a little nervous, and not quite sure what happens between this moment and sitting in your hotel room. This guide walks you through the entire arrival process at Shanghai Pudong (PVG), Beijing Capital (PEK), and Beijing Daxing (PKX). The process is identical at all major Chinese international airports, with only minor layout differences.

Read it once on the plane, and you'll know exactly what's coming.

💡 Do this on the plane
Before landing, the cabin crew hands out a paper arrival card (入境卡). Fill it out on the plane — it saves scrambling at a tiny counter later. You'll need your passport number, flight number, hotel address (write it down or save it offline), and duration of stay. As of 2025-2026, many visa-free nationalities don't need the card at all, but it varies by airport — fill it out if offered.

Step 1 — Walk to immigration

Follow the "Arrivals / 到达 / Immigration" signs. You do not collect bags yet — immigration comes before baggage claim in every Chinese airport. The walk is usually 5-10 minutes and signed in English.

In the immigration hall you'll see two lanes:

  • Chinese Citizens (中国公民): Ignore this.
  • Foreigners (外国人) / Foreign Passports: This is your lane. Some airports also have a dedicated Visa-Free / 240-hour Transit lane — use it if you're on a transit exemption.
⏱️ Typical wait time
Expect 15-45 minutes depending on how many flights landed together. Pudong and Daxing have new automated e-channel booths for foreigners from certain countries — these move faster than manual booths. Late-night arrival is usually 10 minutes or less.

Step 2 — Immigration inspection

At the booth, hand the officer:

  1. Your passport (must be valid 6+ months with at least one blank page)
  2. Your visa (if required — visa-free nationals don't need anything beyond the passport)
  3. The arrival card (if you were given one)

The officer asks a few routine questions, usually in basic English:

  • "What's the purpose of your visit?" — "Tourism" is the easy answer.
  • "How long will you stay?"
  • "Where are you staying?" — have your hotel name and address ready, saved offline.
  • "Do you have a return ticket?" — occasionally asked, rarely checked. A screenshot helps.

Most nationalities now go through biometric capture: a digital photo (no glasses/hat) and two thumbprints at a scanner next to the booth. It takes about 10 seconds. The officer then stamps your passport — that's your entry record.

⚠️ Check the stamp before you walk away
Flip through your passport and confirm you have the entry stamp with a clear date. If you're visa-free, the stamp shows how many days you have (typically 30). If anything looks wrong, ask the officer immediately — fixing it later is much harder.

Step 3 — Baggage claim

Follow the "Baggage Claim / 行李提取" signs. Large screens show your flight number and carousel number. Bags start appearing 15-25 minutes after landing. Free luggage carts are stacked near the entrance to the hall — grab one.

If your bag doesn't arrive, find your airline's baggage desk (near the carousel) with the claim tag sticker from your boarding pass.

Step 4 — Customs

After bags, you walk through customs. Two channels:

  • Green channel (Nothing to declare): Normal tourists use this. Walk through. You may be randomly selected for a bag scan — two minutes.
  • Red channel (Goods to declare): Only if you're carrying over ¥20,000 cash, foreign currency over $5,000 equivalent, or restricted goods. Tourist gear is almost always green.

Step 5 — You're in the arrivals hall. Now what?

This is the critical moment. You need three things before heading to the city: internet, working payment, and transport. If you set up your eSIM and Alipay before flying, you're already done. If not, here are your options in order of what you'll see in the hall:

Free airport WiFi

Connect to "Airport-Free-WiFi" or similar. The captive portal asks for a phone number for an SMS code — many airports now accept foreign numbers. If the SMS doesn't arrive (common with some foreign carriers), look for the passport-scan WiFi machines near the SIM counters — they print a code after you scan your passport.

Tourist SIM counters

China Mobile, China Unicom, and China Telecom have booths in the arrivals hall. Staff speak basic English. Expect ¥100-200 for a 7-day data SIM, and bring your passport for real-name registration. This works but is slower and pricier than an eSIM.

Currency exchange

Change a small amount ($100-200) for emergency cash. Rates at airport counters are noticeably worse than city banks, so don't change more than you need for the first day. ATMs in the city give better rates.

⚠️ Ignore the greeters
After customs you'll walk past people holding signs for "taxi" or "transport" offering rides at high prices. These are not official airport taxis. Keep walking — official taxis, DiDi pickup, and the metro are all signed further ahead.

Step 6 — Getting into the city

Metro / airport train (cheapest)

Pudong (PVG): The Maglev train reaches Longyang Road in 8 minutes (¥50), or Metro Line 2 runs all the way into central Shanghai for ~¥9. Signs in English.

Capital (PEK): Airport Express train to Dongzhimen in 25 minutes (¥25), connects to Metro Lines 2 and 13.

Daxing (PKX): Daxing Airport Express to Caoqiao in 19 minutes (¥35), connects to Line 10.

Good if you're traveling light and your hotel is near a metro stop. Not ideal with 3 suitcases at 11 PM.

DiDi rideshare (best balance)

Open DiDi, type your hotel name in English, confirm your pickup location on the map. Typical costs:

  • Pudong → central Shanghai: ¥150-220 ($20-30), 40-75 minutes depending on traffic
  • Capital → central Beijing: ¥100-150, 40-60 minutes
  • Daxing → central Beijing: ¥150-200, 45-70 minutes

Pay through the app with Alipay or your linked international card. No cash, no Chinese needed.

ℹ️ Where to meet your DiDi
Airports have marked "Ride-Hailing / Online Car-Hailing" pickup zones with English signs. At Pudong it's Parking Garage 2; at Daxing follow the orange "Ride-Hailing" signs. Wait there — the driver won't come into the terminal.

Official taxi rank (safe, slightly more expensive)

Follow "Taxi" signs to a staffed queue. All taxis are metered — no haggling, no negotiating. To central Shanghai from Pudong: ¥180-250. To central Beijing from Capital: ¥100-150. Most accept cash or Alipay/WeChat; international cards are rare in taxis.

Pre-booked hotel transfer (most comfortable)

Mid-range and upscale hotels offer airport pickup for ¥400-800 with an English-speaking driver holding a name sign. Book before leaving home. Worth it if you're arriving at 2 AM with kids or a lot of luggage.

Step 7 — The first 30 minutes at the hotel

Once you're in the room, do these in order:

  1. Connect to hotel WiFi — international hotels usually have open internet (no VPN needed on the hotel network, though speed varies; budget/domestic hotels may not).
  2. Message family you've arrived safely — WhatsApp/WeChat/iMessage works with your eSIM or hotel WiFi.
  3. Verify Alipay works — try sending a ¥1 test transfer to a friend, or buy a bottle of water at a convenience store to confirm your card is linked.
  4. Save the hotel's Chinese address in Amap favorites — you'll show it to taxi drivers all week.
  5. Download DiDi and Amap if you didn't before (you can do this on hotel WiFi without needing your eSIM active).

Quick checklist: before you board

The single biggest thing you can do to make arrival painless is to arrive prepared. Do these three things before you fly:

  1. Buy and install an eSIM — pick from our eSIM comparison guide. 5 minutes, activates on landing.
  2. Set up Alipay — link your Visa/Mastercard before departure. See our Alipay step-by-step.
  3. Save these offline (screenshot or note app): hotel address in Chinese and English, your passport number, flight number, embassy emergency number.

That's it. The entire arrival process — from plane door to hotel room — takes 45-90 minutes depending on immigration wait and your choice of transport into the city. It's well-organized, well-signed in English, and far less intimidating than it looks on a first trip.
