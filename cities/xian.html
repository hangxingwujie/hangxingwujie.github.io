🗿 XI'AN

Xi'an: one great site, one great wall, the best food

CT
City Tech Team
Updated November 2026 · 8 min read
Best for: history + street food Days needed: 2–3 Difficulty: low–moderate Peak season: Apr–May, Sep–Oct

Xi'an has the single best ancient site in China, an intact Ming city wall you can ride a bicycle around, and the most rewarding street food in the country. That combination makes it the most efficient two days in China — you spend less time in transit and more time doing things than in any comparable city.

The one complication is the Terracotta Army: it is 40km east of the city, the ticket system is passport-based, and the site layout punishes anyone who arrives without a plan. Sort that and Xi'an is straightforward.

The Terracotta Army, done properly

Eight thousand life-size soldiers, individually faced, buried in 210 BC and rediscovered by farmers digging a well in 1974. It is not overrated, and the crowds are not exaggerated.

  • Book ahead with your passport. Tickets are sold online against passport details and the daily allocation does go. Book two to three days out; a week in peak season.
  • Go at opening, not at 10am. Doors open around 8:30am. The tour buses arrive between 9:30 and 10:00. Being ninety minutes early is the difference between an empty Pit 1 and a wall of selfie sticks.
  • Visit the pits in reverse order. Most groups go Pit 1 → 2 → 3. Do 3 → 2 → 1 instead and you finish at the big one, by which time the buses have moved on.
  • Pit 1 is the vast one — aircraft-hangar scale, ranks of infantry. Pit 2 has the cavalry and archers. Pit 3 is the command post and small. The bronze chariots in the exhibition hall are worth more time than most people give them.
  • Getting there: Tourist Bus 5 (306) from the east square of Xi'an Railway Station, ¥8, about an hour. Or a DiDi for roughly ¥100–130 each way, which for two people is often worth the hour saved.
⚠️ The fake guide problem
People will approach you at the bus stop and at the site entrance offering "official" English tours. They are not official. Either use the audio guide rented at the entrance (good, cheap, and honest) or arrange a licensed guide through your hotel. Expect aggressive selling in the car park and ignore it.

The city wall: rent a bike

Xi'an's Ming-dynasty wall is 14km around, 12m high, and wide enough to drive a car along the top. It is the best-preserved city wall in China and the best thing to do in Xi'an after the warriors.

  • Bike rental: available on top of the wall at several gates, roughly ¥45 for two hours with a deposit. Tandem bikes available.
  • How long: a full circuit is about 90 minutes at an easy pace with photo stops. Two hours is comfortable.
  • Best time: late afternoon into sunset. The wall is exposed with no shade — midday in summer is punishing.
  • Best gate: South Gate (Yongningmen) is the grandest and closest to the centre. Enter there.

The Muslim Quarter, and what to eat

The Muslim Quarter (回民街) around Beiyuanmen Street is a living Hui neighbourhood, not a theme park — though the main drag has become one. The food is the point.

  • Roujiamo (肉夹馍) — slow-braised pork in a flatbread. China's original sandwich, ¥8–15. The single best thing to eat in the city.
  • Biangbiang noodles — belt-wide hand-pulled noodles with chilli oil. The character for "biang" has 58 strokes and cannot be typed on most phones.
  • Yangrou paomo (羊肉泡馍) — lamb soup with torn flatbread. You tear the bread yourself; the smaller you tear it, the better the result. A ritual as much as a meal.
  • Persimmon cakes, sesame sweets, grilled skewers — graze rather than commit to one restaurant.
  • Where to go: the main Beiyuanmen strip is crowded and overpriced. Walk one block west into Dapiyuan or the lanes off Sajinqiao for the same food at half the price and no queue.
💡 Comes alive after dark
The Muslim Quarter is a night market. Arrive around 6pm when the lights come on and the local families come out. At 11am it is a row of closed shutters.

Two days, realistically

1
Day 1 morning — the warriors

Leave the hotel at 7am, at the site for opening, pits in reverse order. Back in the city by early afternoon.

2
Day 1 late afternoon — the wall

Rent a bike at the South Gate around 4pm, ride the full circuit into sunset.

3
Day 1 evening — the Muslim Quarter

Eat your way down Beiyuanmen, then branch into Dapiyuan. This is dinner and entertainment in one.

4
Day 2 morning — Big Wild Goose Pagoda

The Tang-dynasty pagoda and the surrounding squares. The fountain show in the north square runs in the evening and is free.

5
Day 2 afternoon — Shaanxi History Museum

Free but reservation-only with a passport, and genuinely one of the best museums in China. Book ahead. Closed Mondays.

6
Optional extra day

Mount Hua (华山), one of China's five sacred mountains, about 30 minutes by high-speed rail. Plank walkways, vertical ladders, serious hiking. A full day and not for anyone uneasy with heights.

Practical notes

  • Getting there: Xi'an is 4.5–6 hours from Beijing and 6–7 from Shanghai by high-speed rail. The station (Xi'an North) is connected to the metro. See booking rail tickets.
  • Metro: nine lines, cheap, English-signed, Alipay QR accepted. Line 2 runs north–south through the centre and connects to Xi'an North station.
  • Terracotta Army is not on the metro. Bus 306 or DiDi, as above.
  • Winter is cold and dry (around 0°C); summer is genuinely hot (35°C+) and the wall has no shade.
  • Air quality can be poor in winter — check the AQI before planning a long outdoor day.

Xi'an checklist

  1. Book Terracotta Army tickets with your passport, 2–3 days ahead minimum.
  2. Be at the site for opening — not 10am.
  3. Do the pits in reverse order (3 → 2 → 1).
  4. Reserve the Shaanxi History Museum; it is free but capacity-limited and closed Mondays.
  5. Ride the city wall late afternoon, from the South Gate.
  6. Eat in Dapiyuan, not on the main Beiyuanmen strip.
  7. Carry your passport — you need it for tickets, museums and hotel check-in.
