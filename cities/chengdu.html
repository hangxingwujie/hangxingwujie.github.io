🐼 CHENGDU

Chengdu: the one city where you should slow down

CT
City Tech Team
Updated November 2026 · 9 min read
Best for: pandas, food, pace Days needed: 2–3 Difficulty: low Peak season: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov

Chengdu is the exception to the way most of China travels. Everywhere else rewards efficiency — see more, move faster, book earlier. Chengdu rewards the opposite. The city's entire identity is built around sitting down: in a teahouse, over a long lunch, at a hotpot table for three hours.

That makes it the best place in the country to recover from travelling in China, and the best base for the mountains of western Sichuan if you want to extend.

The pandas: timing is everything

The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, north of the city, is the reason most people come. It is also where most visitors get it wrong.

⚠️ Pandas sleep through the afternoon
Giant pandas are most active between roughly 8am and 10:30am, when they are fed. By 11am most are asleep in a heap and stay that way. Arriving at 10am means watching sleeping animals behind glass in a crowd.
  • Arrive for opening — around 7:30–8:00am depending on season. This is the single most important decision of your Chengdu trip.
  • Book tickets online with your passport in advance, especially at weekends and holidays.
  • Route: walk to the furthest enclosures first (the nursery and the cub area), then work back towards the entrance as the crowds build. Most people do the opposite.
  • Getting there: metro Line 3 to Panda Avenue station, then a short shuttle bus. Or DiDi, about ¥50 from the centre and 30 minutes.
  • Alternative: Dujiangyan Panda Base is quieter and further out; it also runs volunteer programmes. Worth it if you have a spare day.

Teahouses: how to actually do it

Chengdu has thousands of teahouses and they are not a tourist performance — they are where locals spend entire afternoons playing mahjong, getting their ears cleaned, and arguing about nothing.

  • People's Park (人民公园) is the classic introduction. The Heming Teahouse there is famous and busy; the smaller ones in the same park are just as good and half the price.
  • What to order: jasmine tea (花茶) is the local default, served in a lidded glass or porcelain cup with a thermos of hot water to top it up yourself. Refills are free and unlimited.
  • How long to stay: two hours minimum. Staying one hour misses the point.
  • Ear cleaning (采耳) — a practitioner with tuning forks and long tools cleans your ears in public for around ¥50. It is a genuine Chengdu ritual, odd at first, oddly relaxing.
  • Cost: ¥15–40 for tea. That buys you a seat for the whole afternoon.
💡 This is the best value in China
A ¥25 pot of tea and four hours of people-watching is the cheapest, most authentically Chengdu thing you can do. It is also the thing most foreign visitors skip in favour of another temple.

Eating in Chengdu

Sichuan food abroad is not Sichuan food. The real thing is numbing (麻) before it is hot (辣), and it uses far more variation than the "extra spicy" caricature.

  • Hotpot — the beef-tallow version, split pot if you need it. ¥80–150 per person.
  • Mapo tofu (麻婆豆腐) — the definitive dish. Numbing, not just hot. ¥20–30.
  • Dan dan noodles (担担面) — small bowl, intense flavour, ¥10–15. A breakfast food here.
  • Kung pao chicken (宫保鸡丁) — sweet, sour, numbing, nothing like the Western version.
  • Chuanchuan (串串香) — skewers you pick yourself and cook in a shared pot, charged per stick. Cheaper and more social than hotpot.
  • Where: Yulin district has the best concentration of small restaurants. Kuanzhai Alley and Jinli are restored tourist streets — pretty, overpriced, not where you eat.
⚠️ If you cannot handle chilli
Say "bú yào là" (不要辣) and it will still arrive with some. Ask for "wēi là" (微辣, mildly spicy) instead — it gets you closer to what you actually want. Also: Sichuan pepper numbs your mouth, which is a different sensation from heat, and it is the part most people do not expect.

Two days, realistically

1
Day 1 dawn — pandas

At the gate for opening. Two to three hours. Back in the city by late morning.

2
Day 1 afternoon — People's Park teahouse

Tea, ear cleaning, mahjong watching. Do nothing deliberately for three hours.

3
Day 1 evening — hotpot

Yulin district. Order a split pot, point at pictures, take your time.

4
Day 2 morning — Wenshu Monastery

A working Buddhist monastery with a very good vegetarian restaurant and a tea garden. Far calmer than the tourist temples.

5
Day 2 afternoon — Jinsha or Sichuan Museum

The Jinsha site museum covers the ancient Shu kingdom and the famous gold sun bird — genuinely impressive and rarely crowded.

6
Optional extra days

Chengdu is the gateway to western Sichuan: Leshan Giant Buddha (1 hour by rail), Mount Emei (2 hours), Jiuzhaigou (flight or long bus), or the Sichuan-Tibet highway if you have real time.

Practical notes

  • Metro: 13 lines, cheap, English-signed, Alipay QR accepted. Line 2 and Line 3 cover most visitor ground.
  • Getting there: high-speed rail from Xi'an in 3–4 hours, from Chongqing in about 1.5 hours. Chengdu has two airports; Tianfu (TFU) is the newer and larger one, further out.
  • Weather: famously overcast and humid. Chengdu gets little direct sun year-round, which is not a reason to skip it. Summer is hot and sticky; winter is grey and mild.
  • Pace: do not over-schedule. Two sights a day is right here; four is a mistake.

Chengdu checklist

  1. Book panda base tickets online with your passport.
  2. Be at the gate for opening — afternoon pandas are asleep.
  3. Walk to the far enclosures first, work back.
  4. Put aside three hours for a teahouse. Genuinely.
  5. Book a hotpot dinner in Yulin, not on Jinli or Kuanzhai Alley.
  6. Learn "wēi là" (微辣) rather than "bú yào là".
  7. Leave a spare day for Leshan or Emei if you have it.
