🌉 CHONGQING

Chongqing: a city with no flat ground

CT
City Tech Team
Updated November 2026 · 9 min read
Best for: urban spectacle, food Days needed: 2 Difficulty: moderate–high Peak season: Mar–May, Sep–Nov

Chongqing is built on hills so steep that the city has effectively given up on flat. Roads stack four levels deep, a monorail runs straight through a residential tower, and the ground floor of one building is the eleventh floor of another. Google Maps cannot cope with it, and neither can most first-time visitors.

That disorientation is the attraction. Chongqing is the most cinematic city in China, and it is also the only one where the metro system will genuinely baffle you. Here is how to deal with it.

First, understand the vertical logic

Chongqing is where the Yangtze meets the Jialing, and the city is draped over the ridges between them. Three consequences for anyone visiting:

  • "Ground floor" is meaningless. A building's lobby may be at street level on one side and 40 metres up on the other. Always check which entrance you want before setting off.
  • Metro exits are not at street level. An exit labelled "Exit 3" can put you ten floors above or below your destination. The metro map is accurate about lines and useless about altitude.
  • Straight-line distance lies. Two points 300m apart on a map can be a 15-minute walk involving stairs, escalators and a footbridge. Always use a walking route in Amap or Baidu Maps — never estimate from distance.
⚠️ Wear the right shoes
This is not a city for sandals or anything with a heel. You will climb stairs constantly, often in wet weather. Chongqing's nickname is "mountain city" for entirely literal reasons.

The monorail through a building

Liziba station (李子坝) on Line 2 is where the monorail passes through the middle of a 19-storey residential block — floors 6 to 8 are the station, and the residents above and below carry on as normal. It is free to watch from the street.

  • Where to stand: there is a purpose-built viewing platform opposite the station, below and to the side. Follow the crowd.
  • When: trains run every few minutes, so timing is not critical. Late afternoon light is best for photographs.
  • Ride it too: Line 2 between Liziba and Jiaochangkou is one of the most scenic metro rides anywhere, running along the cliff above the river.

Getting around without losing your mind

  • Metro: 12 lines, ¥2–10, good English signage. Alipay QR works — set it up before you arrive.
  • Critical detail: check the exit letter and the exit altitude on the station map. The station layout diagrams show which exits are at which level. This single habit saves hours.
  • Light rail vs monorail: Lines 2 and 3 are elevated and scenic. Line 6 crosses the Jialing on a spectacular bridge.
  • DiDi works well and is often easier than the metro for hillside destinations, but drivers may call you to ask which level you are on. Have your hotel's Chinese name and address ready to show.
  • Taxis are cheap (flagfall ¥10) and plentiful. Show the destination in Chinese characters.

Hotpot: the reason you came

Chongqing hotpot is a different animal from the Beijing version. Beef tallow, a alarming quantity of Sichuan peppercorn and dried chilli, and a divided pot so the timid can eat too. It is numbing, not just spicy.

  • Ordering: pick a split pot (鸳鸯锅) — half mild, half fiery — unless you are confident. Then order plates: sliced beef, tripe, luncheon meat, tofu skin, potato, lotus root. Everything is cooked by you at the table.
  • The dipping sauce: sesame oil with garlic, not peanut sauce. That is the local way and it exists to cool the chilli.
  • Cost: ¥80–150 per person with drinks at a normal local place. The famous chains are fine; the ones with plastic chairs and no English menu are better.
  • Language barrier: most hotpot menus have pictures. Point. It works.
  • Afterwards: your clothes will smell of chilli oil for a day. This is unavoidable and part of the experience.

Two days, realistically

1
Day 1 afternoon — Ciqikou

The old town on the Jialing, restored but still inhabited. Crowded on weekends. Good for snacks and tea houses; two hours is enough.

2
Day 1 evening — Hongyadong and hotpot

See Hongyadong lit up from Qiansimen Bridge, then eat hotpot nearby. This is the essential Chongqing evening.

3
Day 2 morning — Liziba and Line 2

Watch the monorail pass through the building, then ride Line 2 along the river.

4
Day 2 afternoon — cableway and Nanshan

Cross the Yangtze by cable car, then up to Nanshan for the panorama at dusk.

5
Optional extra day — Dazu Rock Carvings

A UNESCO site of Buddhist cliff carvings about 2 hours away. Genuinely extraordinary and almost no foreign visitors. Full day.

Chongqing checklist

  1. Install Amap — Google Maps is unusable here and will send you to the wrong level.
  2. Set up Alipay QR for the metro before you arrive.
  3. Check metro exit altitude, not just exit letter.
  4. Pack shoes you can climb stairs in.
  5. Save your hotel's Chinese name and address — drivers will ask which level.
  6. See Hongyadong after 7pm, from across the road, not inside.
  7. Eat hotpot at least once, with a split pot if you are unsure.
