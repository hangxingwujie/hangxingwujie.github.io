🥟 GUANGZHOU

Guangzhou: the most underrated city on this list

CT
City Tech Team
Updated November 2026 · 9 min read
Best for: food, Hong Kong access Days needed: 2 Difficulty: low–moderate Peak season: Oct–Dec

Guangzhou gets skipped. Most itineraries run Beijing–Xi'an–Shanghai and stop, which is a mistake, because Guangzhou has the best food culture in China, a genuinely pleasant subtropical riverfront, and — practically — the fastest route from the mainland into Hong Kong.

One thing to know before you arrive: this is a Cantonese city first. The local language is Cantonese, Mandarin is second, and English is thinner on the ground than in Shanghai. That is part of the appeal, but plan for it.

Morning tea is a ritual, not a meal

Yum cha (饮茶) — literally "drink tea" — is the defining Guangzhou experience. It happens in the morning, it involves dim sum, and it lasts two hours. It is not breakfast in the Western sense and you should not treat it as a quick stop.

  • Timing: traditionalists go at 7–8am. Restaurants serve it until around 11am, then switch to lunch menus. Weekend mornings involve queues.
  • How it works: you sit, order tea first (jasmine, pu'er, or chrysanthemum), and then order dim sum either from a cart or a picture menu you tick off with a pencil. The bill is calculated per steamer basket.
  • Tap the table with two fingers when someone pours your tea. It is a silent thank-you with a specific origin story and locals notice when foreigners do it.
  • What to order: har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai (pork and shrimp), char siu bao (barbecue pork buns), cheung fun (rice noodle rolls), chicken feet if you are brave, egg tarts to finish.
  • Cost: ¥60–120 per person for a serious spread. The grand old teahouses cost more than neighbourhood places and are not necessarily better.
💡 Where to go
Diandude (点都德) is the reliable modern chain, English-friendly, and busy. For the old-school experience, look for any large restaurant with round tables and elderly locals reading newspapers — that is the signal you want. Avoid hotel dim sum; it is twice the price and half the atmosphere.

Cantonese food beyond dim sum

  • Roast goose (烧鹅) — the real local obsession, richer than duck. Order it at any Cantonese barbecue restaurant.
  • White cut chicken (白切鸡) — poached, served cold with ginger-scallion oil. Deceptively plain, technically demanding, excellent.
  • Wonton noodles (云吞面) — thin egg noodles in a clear shrimp-roe broth. ¥15–25 and a complete meal.
  • Claypot rice (煲仔饭) — rice cooked in a clay pot with toppings, crisp crust at the bottom. A winter dish, and worth seeking out.
  • Double-skin milk (双皮奶) — a warm set milk dessert. Order it. It sounds strange and tastes like the best custard you have had.
  • Where: Shangxiajiu and Beijing Road for the well-known strips; Xiguan old neighbourhood for the better, cheaper version of the same food.

The Hong Kong connection

Guangzhou South Railway Station is one of the largest in Asia and puts you in central Hong Kong in under an hour.

  • High-speed rail to West Kowloon: roughly 47–58 minutes, ¥215–247 second class. Immigration for both sides is handled at West Kowloon station, so you clear mainland exit and Hong Kong entry in one place.
  • Book ahead: this is a popular route and seats do sell out at weekends and holidays. See booking rail tickets.
  • Visa note: entering Hong Kong is a separate immigration jurisdiction with its own rules for most nationalities — check your own entry requirements, they are frequently different from mainland China's.
  • Also worth knowing: Macao is about an hour away by high-speed rail to Zhuhai, then a short walk across the border.

What is actually worth your time

PlaceTimeWhy
Shamian Island2 hoursFormer colonial concession, wide tree-lined avenues, banyan trees, crumbling European buildings. The calmest, most walkable part of the city and almost empty on weekdays.
Canton Tower2 hoursThe city's landmark, 600m, with an observation deck and a bubble tram on the roof. Go at dusk. Book online for a discount.
Chen Clan Ancestral Hall1.5 hoursThe best example of Lingnan architecture in the city — ornate roof ridges, wood carving, ceramic figurines. Free or nearly so, and genuinely impressive.
Pearl River night cruise1.5 hoursUnlike Shanghai, this one is worth it — the riverfront lighting is the attraction and there is no cheap public ferry substitute that covers the same ground.
Yongqing Fang1.5 hoursRestored Xiguan old lanes turned into cafés, shops and small museums. Tourist-friendly but done well. Good for an afternoon. Closed Mondays.

Two days, realistically

1
Day 1 morning — yum cha

A proper two-hour dim sum breakfast. Do not schedule anything immediately after.

2
Day 1 afternoon — Xiguan and Shamian

Old neighbourhood lanes, then walk across to Shamian Island for the trees and the quiet.

3
Day 1 evening — Pearl River

Canton Tower at dusk, then dinner in Shangxiajiu or a Cantonese barbecue place in Xiguan.

4
Day 2 morning — Chen Clan Hall

Then Yongqing Fang nearby. Both are compact and close together.

5
Day 2 afternoon — choose

Either continue into Hong Kong by high-speed rail (47 minutes), or take a day trip, or slow down over another long meal — which is the more Guangzhou thing to do.

Practical notes

  • Airport: Baiyun (CAN), well connected by metro Line 3 to the centre in about 45 minutes for ¥10-ish. Taxis ¥120–150.
  • Metro: 16 lines, English-signed, Alipay QR accepted. Clean and efficient; Line 1 and Line 3 cover most visitor areas.
  • Language: Cantonese first. Many older shopkeepers and taxi drivers are more comfortable in Cantonese than Mandarin, and English is limited. A translation app is more useful here than in Shanghai.
  • The Canton Fair (April and October) books out the entire city and triples hotel prices. Check the dates before you plan — this is the one scheduling constraint that really matters in Guangzhou.
  • Weather: subtropical. Hot and wet May–September, with typhoon season in summer. October–December is the good window.
  • Canton Fair note: if you are in town for business rather than tourism, plan accommodation two to three months ahead.

Guangzhou checklist

  1. Do one proper two-hour yum cha. Not a quick breakfast.
  2. Check Canton Fair dates — if they overlap your trip, book hotels months ahead.
  3. Install a translation app; English is thinner here.
  4. Walk Shamian Island on a weekday morning.
  5. Book the Hong Kong high-speed rail in advance if that is your onward leg.
  6. Check Hong Kong's own entry requirements separately — they are not mainland rules.
  7. Eat roast goose at least once.
